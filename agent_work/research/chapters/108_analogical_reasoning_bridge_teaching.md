## CVIII.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-BRIDGE already kills bridge-count ads and re-explain≡bridge. The open product question is *how* to teach the join once diagnosed. Edtech defaults: (1) mention an analogy in coach copy, (2) skin both sides with the same story franchise, (3) dump a fluent “this is just like…” monologue, (4) celebrate “higher-order thinking” without a mechanism. Cognitive science is sharper: **analogy is structural alignment** — mapping systems of relations — and classrooms routinely under-support the mapping even when they “use analogies.”

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** MindCraft’s commercial wedge (“the gap is the connection”) only ships if Practice forces *noticed structure* across the join — not if Map draws pretty edges and chat narrates likeness.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. Analogy Score™ / Mapping Minutes / Higher-Order Thinking Score™ as learning NS.  
2. Analogy *mention* or story-skin match ≡ analogy *learning*.  
3. Surface similarity (same characters, same numbers vibe) ≡ relational alignment.  
4. AI monologue “this is like X” without student-produced mapping.  
5. Always-analogy dogma on every item (load + freeze risk).  
6. Cross-domain metaphor cosplay as ACT readiness.  
7. Discord/forum “peer analogy share” ≡ supported comparison.  
8. Analogy kits / “we teach with analogies” ACT-point guarantees.

## CVIII.3 Structure-mapping: relations, not attributes

**FACT (theory):** Gentner (1983, *Cognitive Science, 7*(2), 155–170, doi:10.1207/s15516709cog0702_3) — structure-mapping: analogy interprets a base–target pair by mapping **relations among objects** (and preferring systematic higher-order relational systems), not by matching object attributes; syntax of the representation, not domain content alone, constrains the mapping.

**FACT (review extension):** Gentner & Markman (1997, *American Psychologist, 52*(1), 45–56, doi:10.1037/0003-066X.52.1.45) — analogy and similarity share structural alignment; comparison highlights common relational structure and can generate candidate inferences from the aligned system.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Bridge UX must ask for *role correspondence* (“what plays the role of the slope here?” / “which quantity is the conserved quantity across both?”) — not “these look similar” or “same story world.” Densifies SAFE-BRIDGE remediation of the *relation*.

**Kill:** Attribute/story match as bridge proof.  
**Survive:** Student-named relational alignment as the learning act.

## CVIII.4 Spontaneous transfer fails without noticing the structure

**FACT (classic):** Gick & Holyoak (1980, *Cognitive Psychology, 12*(3), 306–355, doi:10.1016/0010-0285(80)90013-4) — learners who possessed an analogous “fortress” story often failed to apply the convergence principle to Duncker’s radiation problem until a **hint** made the connection available. Structural isomorphism does not guarantee noticed transfer.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Map edge callouts and Practice bridge missions are the product “hint” — they must cue *search for the join*, not re-teach an endpoint. Densifies SAFE-BRIDGE / SAFE-TRANSFER; pairs with SAFE-PREDICT when the student predicts which mapping holds before reveal.

**Kill:** Coverage of both sides ≡ transfer readiness.  
**Survive:** Designed noticing + mapping prompts on the join.

## CVIII.5 Analogical encoding: compare two cases, don’t study them apart

**FACT (experiments):** Gentner, Loewenstein, & Thompson (2003, *Journal of Educational Psychology, 95*(2), 393–408, doi:10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393) — analogical encoding (drawing a comparison across examples) improved schema abstraction and transfer vs no case study; **comparing two cases beat studying the two cases separately**; increasing comparison support raised transfer in a dynamic negotiation exercise.

**FACT (earlier line):** Loewenstein, Thompson, & Gentner (1999, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 6*(4), 586–597, doi:10.3758/BF03212967) — comparing two cases during study made transfer of a negotiation principle far more likely than presenting the same cases separately.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Default bridge Practice should be **side-by-side compare** (two join instances or endpoint→join pair) with an explicit “what is the same structure?” SE — not sequential node drills that happen to mention both concepts. Densifies SAFE-SE and SAFE-GENERATE (student produces the schema language).

**Kill:** Separate restudy of A then B as bridge remediation.  
**Survive:** Forced compare across co-present cases as the encoding move.

## CVIII.6 Classroom math: analogy mention ≠ supported analogy

**FACT (TIMSS video):** Richland, Zur, & Holyoak (2007, *Science, 316*(5828), 1128–1129, doi:10.1126/science.1142103) — U.S., Hong Kong, and Japanese eighth-grade lessons all used relational comparisons frequently, but **U.S. teachers provided fewer cognitive supports** (familiar source; visual source kept visible; spatial cues; gestures; imagery) that help students notice the mapping.

**FACT (agenda):** Richland, Stigler, & Holyoak (2012, *Educational Psychologist, 47*(3), 189–203, doi:10.1080/00461520.2012.667065) — many students treat math as memorized procedures rather than a relational system; refining instruction so students draw connections through **relational comparisons** is a research-backed path; U.S. classrooms under-use the supports that make comparisons work.

**FACT (cuing RCT grain):** Richland & McDonough (2010, *Contemporary Educational Psychology, 35*(1), 28–43, doi:10.1016/j.cedpsych.2009.09.001) — in two math contexts, providing comparative gestures and **visibly aligned** source/target during an instructional analogy (vs same analogs without such cues) improved later discrimination among potential analogs and reduced susceptibility to misleading surface features — immediately and after a one-week delay. Mere inclusion of a comparison without high support was not enough for expert-like discrimination.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Bridge cards and Solver soft-wrongs must: (1) co-present both representations, (2) highlight correspondence (spatial alignment / arrows / shared labels), (3) invite comparative gesture or “point what maps” (SAFE-GESTURE optional), (4) then encode with short SE — not dump a second worked example. Densifies SAFE-DUAL (contiguous structural figures) and SAFE-BRIDGE BRIDGE-4.

**Kill:** “We use analogies” brand without co-presence + cues.  
**Survive:** High-support compare as the default bridge remediator.

## CVIII.7 Higher-order thinking is relational reasoning — with supports

**FACT (review):** Richland & Simms (2015, *WIREs Cognitive Science, 6*, 177–192, doi:10.1002/wcs.1336) — analogical/relational reasoning (align, compare, map structured relationships) is a productive cognitive underpinning of “higher-order thinking”; in math/science/history, expert-like knowledge treats concepts as systems of relationships; educational analogies require **explicit support** so learners notice relational relevance, have processing resources to hold relations, and recognize both similarities and differences (where the analogy breaks).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Parent/school copy may say “relational thinking” only when the product instruments mapping acts and join transfer — never Higher-Order Thinking Score™. Flag where the analogy breaks (SAFE-MISCON / soft-wrong contrast) so students do not over-map attributes.

**Kill:** HOT Score™ / Bloom-verbs theater as analogy pedagogy.  
**Survive:** Supported relational mapping + break-points named.

## CVIII.8 Load, expertise, and when NOT to force analogy

**HYPOTHESIS (product seam):** Always-analogy is CLT and anxiety risk. Gate high-support compare to: (1) detected bridge gaps (SAFE-BRIDGE operational definition), (2) FormatId hops that share concept but not vessel (SAFE-FORMAT), (3) near-miss interleaves that share surface and differ on join (XXXIX). Soften when: novice acquisition needs a short worked example before dual display (SAFE-EXPTIME), SAFE-RETRIEVE freeze is active, or dual panels raise abandon (dose supports; sequential with source still visible beats disappearing source).

**Contradiction to watch:** Dual displays can overwhelm; surface-rich story worlds can lure attribute mapping (SAFE-STORYLOAD). Red Team: if high-support compare raises quit without delayed join transfer, narrow to bridge-severity missions only; keep ban on Analogy Score™ and mention≡pedagogy.

**Kill:** Always-analogy dogma / lore-as-structure.  
**Survive:** Severity- and mode-gated compare with load-honest supports.

## CVIII.13 Doctrine — SAFE-ANALOG (provisional)

1. **Relations over attributes** — map roles and systematic structure; ban story/attribute match as proof (Gentner; Gentner & Markman).  
2. **Notice the join** — design the hint; coverage ≠ transfer (Gick & Holyoak; SAFE-BRIDGE).  
3. **Encode by compare** — co-present cases; comparing beats separate study (Gentner et al. 2003).  
4. **Supports required** — source visible; spatial alignment; optional comparative gesture; student alignment act (Richland et al. 2007/2010).  
5. **Name the break** — where the analogy fails; prevent overmap (Richland & Simms; SAFE-MISCON).  
6. **Mode gates** — bridge/format/near-miss severity; soften under freeze and novice EXPTIME load.  
7. **Densify, don’t fork** — BRIDGE diagnosis, TRANSFER measurement, DUAL contiguity, GESTURE optional, PREDICT commit-before-reveal on mappings.  
8. **No Analogy Score™ / mention≡pedagogy / surface-story cosplay / AI likeness dump / always-analogy dogma / analogy ACT ads**.

**Confidence:** High that structure-mapping and supported comparison are real levers (Gentner line; Richland line; Gick & Holyoak). Medium that SPA dual-panel bridge cards will raise delayed join transfer without quit — instrument ANALOG-* before analogy marketing. High confidence to kill Analogy Score™, mention≡pedagogy, and surface-story≡structure regardless.

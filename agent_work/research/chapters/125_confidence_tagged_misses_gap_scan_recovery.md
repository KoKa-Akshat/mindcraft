## CXXV.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-CALIB already bans “raise confidence” as North Star. SAFE-MONITOR already requires judgment→control wiring. SAFE-TAXON already requires typed marks. Gap-scan still tempts a third costume: **Confidence Identity Score™** — mash hard/kinda/easy into a splash (“You’re a Confident Explorer!”), or recover every miss with the same pep + same drill regardless of whether Maya was *sure* and wrong or *guessing* and wrong.

MindCraft already maps confidence to synthetic seed events and level recommendations. That is the right *family* — **if** recovery after gap-scan (and after probe misses) is **class-conditioned**: high-conf miss → attended springboard / hypercorrection path; low-conf miss → scaffold / lack path; low-conf correct → earned challenge unlock; and none of those classes mint an Identity Score™.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Gap-scan’s commercial value is not a confidence vibe. It is a **recovery queue**: which theories to confront, which absences to teach, which “easy” ratings to distrust until FEI proves them — with honest uncertainty until delayed evidence arrives (SAFE-COLD / SAFE-FORGET).

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. Identity Score™ / Confidence Personality™ / “math person type” from hard/kinda/easy.  
2. All gap-scan misses recover the same (one red-X → one generic review deck).  
3. hard/kinda/easy alone ≡ calibrated mastery or ACT readiness (SAFE-COLD).  
4. Confidence slider without mark type / route (Foster CA-alone null — Part L).  
5. Inflate confidence as recovery therapy; Belief Score™ as NS.  
6. Immediate surety ≡ durable JOLs for future recall (SAFE-MONITOR).  
7. Hide-correctness forever = never teach; recovery without attended feedback.  
8. ACT / grade guarantees from confidence-tagged recovery packaging.

## CXXV.3 CRI: certainty separates misconception from don’t-know

**FACT (Certainty of Response Index):** Hasan, Bagayoko, & Kelley (1999, *Physics Education, 34*(5), 294–299, doi:10.1088/0031-9120/34/5/304) — pair MC answers with a Certainty of Response Index; **low certainty** (regardless of correctness) indicates guessing / lack of knowledge; **high certainty + incorrect** indicates a **misconception** (firm belief in a flawed idea), not mere absence.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** MindCraft’s hard/kinda/easy and item surety are CRI-class signals. Recovery must **fork**:
- **High-conf × wrong** → treat as bug/misconception candidate → SAFE-DISTRACTOR option theory if MC → springboard + Map route (SAFE-MISCON / SAFE-TAXON).  
- **Low-conf × wrong** → treat as lack/guess → lighter confrontation, stronger scaffold / fade protect (SAFE-FADE / SAFE-FADEX), teach-then-retry.  
- **Low-conf × correct** → underconfidence class → competence evidence + challenge unlock, not “believe harder” posters (Part L).  
- **High-conf × correct** → provisional strength signal only until delayed FEI (SAFE-COLD / SAFE-FORGET) — never day-one fireworks.

**Kill:** One recovery deck for all reds.  
**Survive:** Class matrix → mission / coach / graph weight.

**Wound:** CRI alone can misclassify “lucky correct with wrong reasons” and “knows but anxious.” Prefer distractor-typed MC + optional brief why when stakes rise (SAFE-DISTRACTOR; multi-tier diagnostics as future densifier — not day-one UX bomb).

## CXXV.4 Hypercorrection — high-conf misses are recovery gold, not shame

**FACT (hypercorrection):** Butterfield & Metcalfe (2001, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 27*(6), 1491–1494, doi:10.1037//0278-7393.27.6.1491) — contrary to interference intuition, **high-confidence errors** were *more* likely to be corrected on retest after feedback than low-confidence errors.

**FACT (attention account):** Butterfield & Metcalfe (2006, *Metacognition and Learning, 1*, 69–84, doi:10.1007/s11409-006-6894-z) — corrective feedback after high-confidence errors captures more attention (tone-detection impairment during feedback); supports surprise/attention mechanism for hypercorrection.

**FACT (partial knowledge / “knew it all along”):** Metcalfe & Finn (2011, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 37*(2), 437–448, doi:10.1037/a0021962) — adults claim “knew it all along” more for high-conf errors; second-guess and cue tasks suggest preferential partial knowledge contributes to easy correction — not mere hindsight theater.

**FACT (children):** Metcalfe & Finn (2012, *Learning and Instruction, 22*(4), 253–261, doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2011.10.004) — Grade 3–6 children hypercorrect high-confidence errors; educationally relevant beyond adult lab samples (HS Maya and younger caseloads).

**FACT (authentic math diagnostic):** Foster, Woodhead, Barton, & Clark-Wilson (2022, *Educational Studies in Mathematics, 109*, 491–521, doi:10.1007/s10649-021-10084-7) — large Eedi dataset (N responses ≈ 219,826; N students = 7,302); confidence correlates with facility; **first evidence for hypercorrection in authentic school mathematics** — higher confidence on an earlier wrong predicted higher odds of later correct on a related quiz after controlling for facility.

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable):** Gap-scan recovery should **prioritize attended feedback** on high-conf misses (and high-ease concepts that later fail probes) — destaked soft-wrong climate (SAFE-ERRCLIMATE), not public humiliation. Low-conf misses need teaching dose first; dumping the same “surprise confrontation” on guesses wastes attention and can raise anxiety (SAFE-FADEX / SAFE-DD).

**Kill:** Shame-the-sure / “how could you be so confident?” brand voice.  
**Survive:** High-conf miss → springboard priority in recovery queue; measure hypercorrection with delayed isomorphic retry, not emoji uplift.

## CXXV.5 Monitoring accuracy is for control — overconfidence ends study too early

**FACT (AIM / retention):** Dunlosky & Rawson (2012, *Learning and Instruction, 22*(4), 271–280, doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2011.08.003) — absolute monitoring accuracy linked to longer-term retention; **overconfidence** can produce premature termination of practice (judging incomplete responses as “correct enough”), undermining delayed recall; improving judgment standards can help.

**Reuse (Part L):** Foster (2021/2022) — CA alone ≈ null on attainment; value is wiring, not the widget.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** After gap-scan, “easy” ratings that collide with probe misses (or later Practice fails) are **overconfidence recovery targets**: do not honor the easy self-report as mastery; schedule FEI prove + spaced revisit (SAFE-SCHED). “Hard” ratings that later show high accuracy with low surety are **underconfidence unlocks**. Recovery UX that celebrates rising confidence without FEI gates re-creates Dunlosky’s premature-termination problem in product form.

**Kill:** Confidence inflation as recovery success metric.  
**Survive:** Judgment→control: restudy / retry / challenge unlock from class, not Belief Score™.

2. **Class before costume** — high-conf wrong ≠ low-conf wrong ≠ blank; fork recovery dose (CRI logic).  
3. **Hypercorrection is a gift** — attended, destaked feedback for sure misses; measure delayed correction, not shame.

5. **No Identity Score™** — never mash hard/kinda/easy into personality / math-person type / Belief Score™.  
6. **Easy ≠ mastered** — honor collision with probes/FEI over self-report fireworks (SAFE-COLD).  
7. **CA is fuel, not attainment engine** — widget without route is theater (Foster null).  
8. **Copy:** “We recover from *how* you missed — sure misconception vs still learning — not from a Confidence Score.” Never “raise your confidence in 10 minutes.”

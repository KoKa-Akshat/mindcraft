MindCraft07 / 13
Why we're not a wrapper

AI content is wrong until it proves otherwise.

Anyone can pipe a chatbot at a student. We built the opposite: a pipeline where every generated lesson has to clear every gate before a student ever sees it. Most attempts don't make it. That is the system working.

fit check→ generate→ render→ structural rubric→ visual + pedagogical gate→ a student sees it
An honest result from our own lab

This week we trained our first model checkpoint. In validation it nailed the format, and got real facts wrong. So it ships nothing. That's not a setback; it's the whole point. The moat isn't the model. It's the refusal to show a student anything unverified.

MindCraft08 / 13
The students the page loses

Not every brain learns from a paragraph.

For a lot of students, ADHD, dyslexic, visual-first thinkers, the wall was never the idea. It was the format: one page of static prose, one shape for every brain. The Desk gives the same idea more than one door.

A simulation

Drag the variable. Watch the idea move. Often that motion is the whole click.

A story

A person, a place, a problem with stakes. The math surfaces; it isn't announced.

A voice

Say it out loud, hear it back. No page at all, if the page is the problem.

A picture

Diagram, graph, number line, the shapes an idea takes when words are in the way.

This isn't a settings toggle. The engine tracks which representation each student actually lands on (diagram, graph, number line, words, symbols) and leads with that one.

We don't claim to treat anything, and we won't. We just stop demanding that a student learn from the one format that already lost them.

MindCraft09 / 13
The market, sized honestly

One campus at a time. On purpose.

Both sides of this marketplace live on the same quad: the student stuck at 2am and the mentor who aced that class last spring. That makes each campus a self-contained experiment we can pass or fail. Falsifiable market math, not a headline TAM.

Now

Macalester

Our home campus, ~2,100 undergrads. Where we recruit the first mentors, run the first sessions, and pitch during Twin Cities Startup Week.

Next

The Twin Cities

A dense cluster of colleges within a few miles, Macalester, St. Olaf, St. Thomas and more, plus the ACT-parent channel already paying $40+/hr for tutoring.

Then

The playbook repeats

Every residential campus has the same shape: stuck students, capable upperclassmen, no marketplace between them. We expand campus by measured campus.

Family subscription, the parent is the buyer Mentor marketplace, platform share of paid sessions Student subscription Institutional licensing, later

Priced against the real next-best alternative: $40+/hr for in-person tutoring, not a hypothetical competitor. We present the model and the unit we'll measure it by; projections come when the first campus produces real numbers.

## CXXVI.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-AAR already says: brief → attempt → structured debrief with objective traces; student speaks first; no military costume; no borrowed meta *d* as ACT marketing. SAFE-LOADSHED already says: under exam surge, shed low-acuity demand so FEI minutes survive. What remains commercially underspecified is the **join under a hard clock**: tutors “save time” by skipping the close, delivering a 40-second lecture (“here’s what you should have done”), or vibes-checking (“how did that feel?”) with zero traces — then ops still claim they “do AARs.”

Competitors resolve the clock wrongly: (1) **skip**; (2) **pour-close** (tutor monologue as fake debrief); (3) **therapy costume**; (4) **bootcamp** roast; (5) **AI wrap essay** that blocks self-analysis. MindCraft’s wedge is a **time-capped micro-debrief** — SHARP-class prompts + Army four questions + objective media — engineered to finish in ~90 seconds without losing the learning function.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Identity transformation needs *reviewed attempts*. Under crunch, the company either designs a faithful short form or it silently abandons review. Skipping the close is not “efficiency” — it is a fidelity breach (SAFE-BURNWARN / SAFE-LOADSHED).

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. “No time for debrief” as acceptable default under crunch.  
2. Tutor/AI lecture that *calls itself* a micro-debrief.  
3. Microdebrief Score™ / Debrief Minutes / Reflection Streak as North Star.  
4. Undifferentiated “science says +25%” / military-cosplay AARs as ACT marketing (SAFE-AAR kill).  
5. Mandatory long therapy-style close when the clock is the constraint.  
6. Vibes-only close without objective traces.  
7. Soft standards / skip-challenge justified by “we at least talked.”  
8. ACT / score guarantees from micro-debrief packaging.

**Operational definition (HYPOTHESIS):** A close is *SAFE-MICRODEB complete* when (a) a **named time cap** is visible (≤90s solo / ≤180s live under load-shed; longer only when acuity warrants and calendar allows), (b) the close runs **four beats minimum**: expected edge → actual (with ≥1 objective trace) → one sustain → one improve-how / next attempt, (c) the **student produces ≥1 self-analytic statement before** tutor/AI wrap, (d) output is a next-attempt commitment logged to Notes/Map — not a grade sermon, (e) under SAFE-LOADSHED the micro-close is on the **protect** list (not an elective to shed), and (f) success = method-change codes / `retry_120s` / `solo_transfer_pass` / brief-open — never Debrief Minutes or Microdebrief Score™.

## CXXVI.3 Debriefs work — transfer the *method*, not the marketing *d*

**FACT:** Tannenbaum & Cerasoli (2013, *Human Factors, 55*(1), 231–245, doi:10.1177/0018720812448394) — meta-analysis of 46 samples (*N* = 2,136): debriefs / AARs improve effectiveness vs control by about **25% on average** (*d* ≈ 0.67); effects similar for teams/individuals and simulated/real settings; **alignment** (participants ↔ focus ↔ measurement) bolsters effects; facilitated/structured debriefs trend stronger.

**FACT:** Keiser & Arthur (2021, *Journal of Applied Psychology, 106*(7), 1007–1032; advance online 2020, doi:10.1037/apl0000821) — bare-bones meta-analysis of 61 studies (107 *ds*; 915 teams and 3,499 individuals): overall AAR effect *d* ≈ **0.79** across multiple training criteria. Two characteristics consistently help: **(a) alignment** to individual vs team, **(b) objective performance review media**. Structure helps especially in military contexts; facilitation interacts with media/alignment.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Under a time cap, protect the two consistent drivers — **alignment** (solo student ↔ solo attempt metrics) and **objective media** (soft-wrong / timing / Map grain) — even if facilitator flourish and long reaction phases are cut. Do **not** protect length-for-length’s sake.

**Kill:** “+25% from debriefs” ACT ads; length≡quality.  
**Survive:** Aligned + traced micro-closes beat skip and vibes-only.  
**Wound:** Meta samples ≠ ACT-anxious Maya — MICRODEB-* must pre-register FEI instruments, not borrow *d*.

## CXXVI.4 Objective review media beat memory theater — even when short

**FACT:** Villado & Arthur (2013, *Journal of Applied Psychology, 98*(2), 292–307, doi:10.1037/a0031510) — 188 individuals in 47 four-person teams; AAR teams outperformed non-AAR teams on team performance, efficacy, communication openness, and cohesion; **objective vs subjective** review media did not significantly differ across five training outcomes in that design — but *having an AAR* beat *no AAR*.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Product should still **prefer** objective traces (Keiser & Arthur consistent moderator) because tutoring memory is biased and tutors under crunch misremember. When traces are missing, micro-debrief still beats skip — but ship attempt cards so “what actually happened” is not a reconstruction fight.

**Kill:** “How did that feel?” as the only close.  
**Survive:** Soft-wrong + timing + named edge as default media for the 90s box.

## CXXVI.5 Time-limited settings need short scaffolds — SHARP as existence proof

**FACT (OSAD quality dimensions):** Arora, Ahmed, Paige, Nestel, Runnacles, Hull, et al. (2012, *Annals of Surgery, 256*(6), 982–988, doi:10.1097/SLA.0b013e3182610c91) — developed and validated the Objective Structured Assessment of Debriefing (OSAD) from literature + 33 end-user interviews across three continents; eight dimensions (approach, learning environment, engagement, reaction, reflection, analysis, diagnosis, application); feasible/reliable (inter-rater ICC ≈ 0.88; test-retest ICC ≈ 0.90).

**FACT (SHARP for the OR clock):** Ahmed, Arora, Russ, Darzi, Vincent, & Sevdalis (2013, *Annals of Surgery, 258*(6), 958–963, doi:10.1097/SLA.0b013e31828c88fc) — prospective pre/post of 100 OR cases (22 trainers, 30 residents): introduced **SHARP** (Set learning objectives; How did it go; Address concerns; Review learning points; Plan ahead) as a **time-feasible** debrief scaffold when full OSAD-depth closes are impractical. Observer OSAD medians rose from 19 (range 8–31) to 33 (range 26–40), *P* < 0.001; users rated usefulness/feasibility high. Companion handbook positions SHARP as the “absolute basics” when there is not enough time for a detailed OSAD-guided debrief (~**5 minutes** class of tool).

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable):** MindCraft’s micro-debrief is the **tutoring analogue of SHARP**, not a watered-down pep talk:
1. **Set** — restate the briefed edge (or name it if brief was skipped — then fix brief next).  
2. **How** — student + trace: expected vs actual.  
3. **Address** — one concern / soft-wrong frame (advocacy–inquiry, not dump).  
4. **Review** — one sustain.  
5. **Plan** — one improve-how / next attempt card.

Under SAFE-LOADSHED, prefer this five-beat micro over canceling reflection so a homework dump can start.

**Kill:** “Full AAR or nothing” purity that becomes skip under crunch; OSAD-full cosplay as session tax.  
**Survive:** Named short scaffold with fidelity checklist; QA samples micro-completeness, not minutes.

**Wound:** Surgical OR ≠ math tutoring — SHARP is method transfer (structure under clock), not brand (“OR-grade tutoring”). MICRODEB-* measures FEI uptake, not OSAD score cosplay.

## CXXVI.6 Good judgment in 90 seconds — advocacy–inquiry, not praise fog or roast

**FACT:** Rudolph, Simon, Dufresne, & Raemer (2006, *Simulation in Healthcare, 1*(1), 49–55, doi:10.1097/01266021-200600110-00006) — “There’s no such thing as ‘nonjudgmental’ debriefing.” **Debriefing with good judgment** pairs advocacy (state observation + evaluative judgment) with inquiry (ask the learner’s frame). Mistakes are puzzles; stance is curiosity without fake nonjudgment.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Micro-debrief scripts default to one advocacy–inquiry pair tied to the soft-wrong code (“I see you flipped the inequality when multiplying by −1 — what were you assuming?”) then stop. Under the cap, **one** deep frame beats three tip dumps (SAFE-FILM / SAFE-EXPLAIN). Praise-fog and roast both burn the clock without FEI.

**Kill:** “Great job!” fog close; public roast close; tip flood.  
**Survive:** One judged observation + one student frame + next attempt.

## CXXVI.8 Doctrine — SAFE-MICRODEB (provisional)

1. **Cap the form, don’t cancel the function** — under time pressure, run micro-AAR; never “no time for debrief” as default (LOADSHED protect).  
2. **Four beats minimum** — expected → actual+trace → sustain → improve-how / next attempt.  
3. **Student speaks first** — ≥1 self-analytic statement before tutor/AI wrap (SAFE-AAR / SAFE-TALK).  
4. **Objective media preferred** — soft-wrong, timing, Map grain; vibes-only is failure mode (Keiser & Arthur; Villado & Arthur).  
5. **SHARP-class scaffold** — Set / How / Address / Review / Plan as the short template (Ahmed et al.) — method not OR cosplay.  
6. **One advocacy–inquiry** — good judgment without lecture flood (Rudolph et al.).  
7. **Alignment** — solo close on solo metrics; don’t measure team vibes with XP (Tannenbaum & Cerasoli).  
8. **Copy:** “Even in a short session, we close the loop on what you tried — in under two minutes.” Never “+25% from military debriefs” or Microdebrief Score™.

**Confidence:** High — debrief metas + SHARP pre/post + OSAD dimensions + Rudolph stance as method sources. Medium — MindCraft-specific 60–90s completeness thresholds and crunch protect-list compliance (needs MICRODEB-*). High — skip/pour/lecture-as-debrief / +25% ACT / Score™ theater as commercially toxic under FEI + SAFE-AAR.

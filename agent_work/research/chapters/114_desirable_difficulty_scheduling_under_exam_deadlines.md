## CXIV.1 Why this chapter exists

Parents buy **exam dates**. Students feel **countdown panic**. Tutors get asked: “Should we keep mixing, or just drill what she knows?” Edtech defaults into two commercially loud failures: (1) **comfort week** — blocked greens / streak theater that feel ready and fail transfer; (2) **cruelty week** — nightly timed floods and parent-visible scores that brand stakes escalation as “desirable difficulty,” disrupting retrieval benefits (Hinze & Rapp) and taxing WM (Ashcraft).

SAFE-DD / SAFE-EXAM / SAFE-SCHED already say: equip then strain, destake to learn / dose to prove, match spacing to horizon. This chapter answers the **calendar join**: *what to hold vs ease as T→0*.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Identity under challenge is built by equipped difficulty across a horizon — not by abandoning challenge in the last week, and not by turning every remaining hour into a mini-ACT.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:** Desirable Difficulty Score™ / Challenge Minutes NS; always-ease-before-ACT / blocked-comfort≡ready; always-max-DD-until-bell; last-48h full timed flood as “science”; cram-week≡ready; guaranteed ACT points from schedule; soft-only forever under deadline.

---

## CXIV.2 Constructs (schedule ≠ stakes ≠ content)

Keep **DD** (equipped challenge), **stakes/pressure dose** (evaluation intensity — not the same as DD), **horizon/RI** (days to the test), and **ISI** (gaps between episodes) distinct. “Ease” can mean less mix (**content ease**) or less simultaneous WM tax via destake/shorter sets/sleep (**affect ease**). Conflating them produces comfort≡ready or never-challenge forever.

**Operational definition (HYPOTHESIS):** SAFE-DDSCHED compliant when (a) far out: spacing + faded interleave + destaked retrieval default, (b) mid-window: **hold mix** while mild pressure doses begin, (c) final ~72h: short spaced retrieval of equipped repertoire + sleep/WM guards + labeled prove probes — not new-grain floods or scored acquisition marathons, (d) readiness = delayed mixed evidence, never blocked greens/cram NPS, (e) no always-ease / always-max / calendar→points marketing.

## CXIV.3 Bjork — difficulty stays desirable only while equipped; deadlines do not repeal that

**FACT (origin / definition):** Bjork (1994) and Bjork & Bjork (2011; see also Bjork & Bjork JARMAC comment lineage) — spacing, variation, interleaving, and testing-as-learning can improve long-term retention and transfer relative to fluent short-term conditions; **many difficulties are undesirable forever**; a difficulty is desirable only when the learner can respond successfully with the processes the difficulty is meant to trigger.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** An approaching ACT date does **not** convert unequipped full mix or high-stakes acquisition into “more science.” If deadline panic unequips (WM tax, sleep loss, new-topic flood), the same schedule that was desirable at T−30d becomes undesirable at T−2d.

**Kill:** Deadline-as-permission to abandon the *equipped* clause.  
**Survive:** Hold DD forms the learner can still answer; change **load and stakes**, not the definition of desirable.

## CXIV.4 Spacing vs cramming under a known test date

**FACT (distributed practice meta):** Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, & Rohrer (2006, *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(3), 354–380, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354) — 839 assessments / 317 experiments; **ISI and retention interval operate jointly**; the ISI that maximizes final retention **increases as the retention interval increases**.

**FACT (temporal ridgeline):** Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted, & Pashler (2008, *Psychological Science, 19*(11), 1095–1102, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x) — optimal gap depends on how long knowledge must last; practical implication: schedule to the **exam horizon**, not a universal “perfect interval.”

**FACT (spacing > cramming; metacognitive trap):** Kornell (2009, *Applied Cognitive Psychology, 23*(9), 1297–1317, doi:10.1002/acp.1537) — spacing (including large-stack / multi-day) beat massing and beat **cramming (massed study on the last day)** for GRE-type pairs; across experiments spacing beat massing for ~90% of participants, yet after first session ~72% *believed* massing was more effective.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Pre-ACT product must resist parent/student demand for last-day massed fluency theater. For short remaining RI, **shorten ISI** (more frequent Returns) — do not collapse to single-night cram as the brand. Densifies SAFE-SCHED / SAFE-DURABLE / SAFE-CRAM.

**Kill:** Cram-week≡ready; “science says grind tonight.”  
**Survive:** Horizon-matched spacing; honesty that fluent last-night massing feels best and often teaches less for delayed/mixed demand.

## CXIV.5 Interleaving prepares cumulative exams — do not trade it for blocked comfort as the readiness story

**FACT (classroom interleave):** Rohrer, Dedrick, & Stershic (2015, *Journal of Educational Psychology, 107*(3), 900–908; ERIC ED557355) — seventh-graders, same problems blocked vs interleaved over ~3 months; interleaved higher on tests 1 and 30 days later (ds ≈ 0.42 / 0.79). Interleaving juxtaposes strategy choice **and** spaces same-kind problems.

**FACT (larger RCT):** Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig, & Cheung (2020, *Journal of Educational Psychology*) — interleaved math practice improved cumulative-test performance vs mostly blocked schedules in a diverse sample over months; exam-like strategy choice is the mechanism (choose method from the problem, not from the worksheet header).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Mid-window, **hold mix** on equipped concepts (ACT-like strategy choice). “Ease” = shorter sets / destake / fewer new grains — not blocked greens sold as ready. Final ~72h may bias known-repertoire retrieval + light mix probes; never redefine blocked accuracy as readiness (SAFE-EXAM).

**Kill:** Always-ease-before-ACT / blocked-comfort≡exam-ready. **Survive:** Hold strategy-choice while equipped; ease load, not the readiness standard.

## CXIV.6 Stakes are not desirable difficulties — pressure during acquisition can erase learning gains

**FACT (retrieval disruption under pressure):** Hinze & Rapp (2014, *Applied Cognitive Psychology, 28*(4), 597–606, doi:10.1002/acp.3032) — high-stakes vs low-stakes quizzes: online quiz performance similar, but **final retention better after low-stakes**; only low-stakes beat reread. Performance pressure can disrupt the memory benefits of retrieval even when session scores look fine.

**FACT (practice tests and anxiety):** Yang, Li, Zhao, Luo, & Shanks (2023, *Educational Psychology Review*, doi:10.1007/s10648-023-09801-w) — practice testing tends to **reduce** test anxiety (Hedges’ g ≈ −0.52 across synthesized studies); easier practice tests more mitigating than difficult ones. Directional support for laddered, not brutal, exam sims.

**FACT (WM under math anxiety):** Ashcraft & Kirk (2001, *JEP: General, 130*(2), 224–237, doi:10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224); Ashcraft & Krause (2007, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14*, 243–248, doi:10.3758/BF03194059) — math anxiety acts like a dual-task load on WM; hard math under threat is resource competition, not moral failure.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Final week: do not make every Practice minute a parent-visible scored ACT. Prefer destaked spaced retrieval for encoding + **labeled prove probes** for pressure. “Easing” means cutting simultaneous stakes + sleep debt + new-grain flood — not deleting mix from the readiness story.

**Kill:** Always-max-DD-until-bell; last-48h scored flood-as-DD. **Survive:** Dual-rail train destaked / prove dosed (Hinze + Ashcraft + Yang).

## CXIV.7 Product law — three horizon bands

**HYPOTHESIS (SAFE-DDSCHED bands):**

| Band | Approx. horizon | Hold | Ease / change | Prove |
|------|-----------------|------|---------------|-------|
| **Build** | T > ~21d | Spacing + faded interleave + destaked retrieval (SAFE-DD/SCHED) | Equip before strain; no day-one flood | Occasional mild timed mix after equip |
| **Join** | ~7–21d | **Hold mix** on equipped path concepts; keep Returns | Shorten sessions if affect high; begin EXPOSE ladder | Rising mild→moderate pressure doses |
| **Protect + probe** | ~0–3d (esp. final 72h) | Short spaced retrieval of **known** repertoire; light mix probes | No new-grain interleave marathons; protect sleep; destake acquisition; no parent scoreboard on train sets | Few labeled timed prove sets — not all-night acquisition |

**Banned patterns:**
1. Always-ease / comfort-week blocked greens as “ready.”  
2. Always-max timed mix every night until the bell.  
3. Cram-week≡ready / overlearn-tonight as method.  
4. Desirable Difficulty Score™ / Challenge Minutes NS.  
5. Schedule→guaranteed ACT points.  
6. Conflating stakes escalation with DD.

**Kill:** Calendar theater extremes.  
**Survive:** Horizon-matched hold of equipped DD + separate pressure dosing.

## CXIV.12 Doctrine — SAFE-DDSCHED (provisional)

1. **Equipped clause never expires** — deadlines do not make unequipped difficulty desirable (Bjork & Bjork).  
2. **Match ISI to remaining RI** — shorten gaps near the exam; do not collapse to last-night massing as method (Cepeda; Kornell).  
3. **Hold mix while equipped** — interleaved strategy choice is exam transfer practice (Rohrer line); ban comfort-week≡ready.  
4. **Stakes ≠ DD** — destake acquisition; dose prove separately (Hinze & Rapp; SAFE-EXAM).  
5. **Ease load, not standards** — final 72h: sleep/WM/new-grain guards + short spaced repertoire; readiness still delayed mixed.  
6. **Laddered practice tests** — anxiety mitigation favors well-sequenced practice over brutal difficulty (Yang et al.).  
7. **WM honesty** — Ashcraft dual-task; do not stack timer + new mix + parent scoreboard + sleep debt.  
8. **Densify, don’t fork** — SAFE-DD / EXAM / SCHED / DURABLE / CRAM / RETRIEVE / EXPOSE.  
9. **No Desirable Difficulty Score™ / Challenge Minutes NS / always-ease / always-max / cram≡ready / schedule ACT guarantees**.

**Confidence:** High that comfort-week and scored-flood calendars are unsafe vs FEI + exam-honest readiness; medium on exact day cutovers (21d/7d/72h) — instrument DDSCHED-* before hardcoding; high to kill Desirable Difficulty Score™ / always-ease≡ready / always-max-until-bell / cram-week≡ready regardless.

## CLIII.3 Inexperienced raters need training — and training ≠ instant interchangeability

**FACT:** Weigle (1994, *Language Testing, 11*(2), 197–223, doi:10.1177/026553229401100206) — verbal protocols of inexperienced ESL composition raters before/after training: training **clarified intended scoring criteria**, **modified expectations**, and provided a **reference group** of peers — hypothesized training effects made empirically visible.

**FACT:** Weigle (1998, *Language Testing, 15*(2), 263–287, doi:10.1177/026553229801500205) — inexperienced vs experienced raters before/after training (FACETS / multi-facet Rasch): before training, inexperienced raters tended to be **more severe and less consistent**; after training, group differences shrank and **consistency improved for most**, but **significant severity differences among raters remained**. Conclusion emphasized: training is more successful at making raters **self-consistent** than at making them **identical** to one another.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Panelists arrive as “inexperienced” on **firm-specific** seals even if they are math SMEs. Loom + quiz can clarify criteria; they do **not** authorize C4. Parallel-run is post-training operational rating with staff as reference — expect residual severity differences; design for **discord + arbitration**, not identical clones.

**Kill:** SOW/Loom≡ready. **Survive:** Training → consistency lift → still need shadow + arbitration. **Wound:** ESL essay scoring ≠ figure IR — borrow *training ≠ interchangeability*, not ESLPE cut scores.

## CLIII.4 Raters are not interchangeable — monitor severity before trusting a new cohort

**FACT:** Engelhard & Myford (2003, *ETS Research Report Series, 2003*(1), RR-03-01, doi:10.1002/j.2333-8504.2003.tb01893.x) — many-facet Rasch analysis of 1999 AP English Literature faculty consultants: raters were **not all interchangeable in severity**; adjusting for severity would have changed AP grades for about **30%** of students (almost all by one grade or less). Monitoring rater behavior is a quality-control problem, not a one-time hire event.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Contract panels need **ongoing** compare-to-staff (shadow then sample dual), not a certificate. Severity on “essential for C4?” and figure–key seals will differ; PATHBLAME must keep `dual_source` visible so ops does not blame “dual” when the mix is new panelists mid-ramp.

**Kill:** “SME credential ≡ interchangeable second rater.” **Survive:** Monitor cohort vs staff gold. **Wound:** Do not import AP grade-adjustment into product UI or marketing.

## CLIII.5 Rater standards drift over a scoring project — parallel-run is also a DRIFT watch

**FACT:** Wolfe, Moulder, & Myford (2001, *Journal of Applied Measurement, 2*(3), 256–280, PMID:12011510) — define **DRIFT** (differential rater functioning over time): rater-by-time interactions including primacy/recency, differential centrality/extremism, practice/fatigue; Rasch procedures can flag some DRIFT types (better power for primacy/recency/centrality/extremism than for practice/fatigue at modest effect sizes).

**FACT:** Myford & Wolfe (2009, *Journal of Educational Measurement, 46*(4), 399–424, doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.2009.00088.x) — framework for **monitoring rater performance over time** (differential accuracy and scale-category use); AP English Literature 2002 data illustrated raters whose accuracy or category use changed as scoring progressed.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Onboarding is not a single gate then forever trust. Shadow catches early severity; **post-graduate sample dual + REVOKE/REPAIRSTRAT** catch mid-spike DRIFT. FILLETA assumes rework if monitors trip — never silently widen authority to clear backlog.

**Kill:** One-and-done certificate. **Survive:** Time-aware monitoring across ramp and surge. **Wound:** MFRM is an ops tool — never Parallel Rasch Score™ splash.

## CLIII.6 Contracting markets undersupply employer training — MindCraft must fund the ramp

**FACT:** Bidwell & Briscoe (2009, *Academy of Management Journal, 52*(6), 1148–1168, doi:10.5465/amj.2009.47084649) — among IT workers, contracting offers **less job security and less employer-provided training** than regular employment; who contracts depends on skills, cues, and (for men) family responsibilities.

**Reuse (SURGEPANEL):** Davis-Blake & Uzzi (1993) — firm-specific skill predicts **against** pure temp externalization; Lepak & Snell (1999) — employment mode should fit uniqueness.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Seals are firm-specific → **paid onboarding + parallel-run hours** belong in CAPPLAN dual-minute cost. Cheap “start tomorrow” panels externalize training risk onto Map. PROCURE bills ramp hours; FILLETA labels `contract_ramp`.

**Kill:** Zero-training contractor dump. **Survive:** Funded ramp as make-or-buy cost. **Wound:** IT contracting ≠ math IR — borrow *training undersupply*, not wage findings.

## CXXIX.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-AAR says **brief → attempt → structured debrief**. SAFE-MICRODEB protects the *close* under a hard clock. What remains underspecified is the **open under a burned clock**: tutors skip (“let’s just start”), pour a monologue catch-up, or vibes-open until the FEI window is gone — then ops still claim they “do briefs.”

Competitors resolve late joins wrongly: (1) **skip** into homework help; (2) **pour-open**; (3) **relationship theater** without naming an edge; (4) **bootcamp** shame-speed; (5) **AI syllabus dump**. MindCraft’s wedge is a **~30s micro-brief** — shared mental model + specific goals + SHARP “Set” — that finishes without losing aim.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Identity transformation needs *aimed attempts*. Skipping the brief is not efficiency — it is a fidelity breach that leaves the later micro-debrief unanchored.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. “No time for brief / goals” as late-join default.  
2. Tutor/AI lecture that *calls itself* a session open.  
3. Brief Score™ / Brief Minutes / Opening Streak as North Star.  
4. Military / surgical / aviation cosplay as ACT marketing.  
5. Mandatory long rapport block when the clock is the constraint.  
6. Vibes-only open without named edge + success criterion.  
7. Soft standards justified by “we at least connected.”  
8. ACT / score guarantees from micro-brief packaging.

## CXXIX.2 Constructs (short ≠ empty; late ≠ aimless)

| Construct | Meaning | MindCraft analogue | Failure mode |
|-----------|---------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Full brief** | Pre-event alignment: intent, success criteria, traps, roles (CRM) | 2–4 min tutor open (SAFE-AAR) | Sermon; fate talk |
| **Micro-brief** | Same function, hard time box, fewer beats | 20–40s solo; ≤60s live late-join | Skip or pour |
| **Late join** | Clock already burned before work starts | Session timer + late flag | Excuse to skip aim |
| **Shared mental model** | Both parties hold same situation / goal / next action | Named edge + trap + coach role | Assumed “you know” |
| **Receiver synthesis** | Student restates plan in own words | “You’re aiming at X; success = Y” | Tutor-only monologue |
| **SAFE-MICROBRIEF** | Capped open fidelity law | This chapter | Brief Minutes NS |

**Operational definition (HYPOTHESIS):** An open is *SAFE-MICROBRIEF complete* when (a) a **named time cap** is visible (≤40s solo / ≤60s live under late-join or LOADSHED; longer only when acuity warrants and calendar allows), (b) the open runs **four beats minimum**: named edge → success criterion → one likely trap / FormatId note → coach role (hint vs silence), (c) the **student produces ≥1 synthesis** (“so today I’m trying…”) before first worked problem, (d) output is a mission/Notes card the micro-debrief can restate — not a syllabus sermon, (e) under SAFE-LOADSHED the micro-open is on the **protect** list (not shed for HW dump), and (f) success = brief_complete × later `retry_120s` / method-change / `solo_transfer_pass` — never Brief Minutes or Brief Score™.

## CXXIX.3 Specific goals beat “do your best” — even in 30 seconds

**FACT:** Locke & Latham (2002, *American Psychologist, 57*(9), 705–717, doi:10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705) — 35-year synthesis: **specific, difficult goals** produce higher task performance than vague, easy, or “do-your-best” goals across laboratory, simulation, and field settings; mechanisms include direction, effort, persistence, and strategy arousal; moderators include commitment, feedback, and task complexity.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** A late-join open that says “let’s just work hard on algebra” is a **do-your-best** failure. A 30-second micro-brief that names “linear inequalities — multiply-by-negative trap; success = one solo transfer without peek” is the tutoring analogue of specific goal setting. Under crunch, **specificity is the time-saver**, not the luxury.

**Kill:** “We’ll figure it out as we go” as default late-join open.  
**Survive:** One named edge + one observable success criterion.  
**Wound:** Organizational task goals ≠ anxious Maya under ACT clock — MICROBRIEF-* must pre-register FEI instruments, not claim Locke *d* as ACT points.

## CXXIX.4 Shared mental models travel — SBAR / I-PASS as method, not hospital cosplay

**FACT:** Haig, Sutton, & Whittington (2006, *Joint Commission Journal on Quality and Patient Safety, 32*(3), 167–175) — case study of **SBAR** (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) as a **shared mental model** tool to standardize clinician communication and reduce style-mismatch failures; pocket-card / phone-cheat-sheet implementation spread beyond single handoffs into reporting culture.

**FACT:** Starmer et al. (2014, *New England Journal of Medicine, 371*(19), 1803–1812, doi:10.1056/NEJMsa1405556) — multicenter prospective intervention of the **I-PASS** handoff bundle across nine hospitals (*N* = 10,740 admissions): medical-error rate ↓23% (24.5 → 18.8 per 100 admissions), preventable adverse events ↓30% (4.7 → 3.3); **oral handoff duration did not increase** (2.4 vs 2.5 min/patient, *P* = 0.55); key elements in written/oral communication rose significantly. Bundle includes mnemonic + training + observation — structure without workflow penalty.

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable):** Late-join tutoring is a **handoff into a truncated shift**. Transfer the *method*:
1. **Situation** — clock state / late flag / remaining minutes.  
2. **Background** — last edge / soft-wrong / Map grain (one card, not biography).  
3. **Assessment** — acuity: FEI practice vs HW triage (SAFE-HWHELP dual-rail).  
4. **Recommendation / action** — today’s edge + success criterion + coach role.  
5. **Synthesis by receiver** — student restates (I-PASS critical element).

Starmer’s non-finding on duration is commercially load-bearing: **structure ≠ longer**. Skip is not required to “save time.”

**Kill:** Hospital/aviation brand cosplay; “I-PASS tutoring raises ACT 23%.”  
**Survive:** Shared-model micro-open with student synthesis; fidelity without minute inflation.  
**Wound:** Medical error rates ≠ transfer_pass — method transfer only.

## CXXIX.5 Time-boxed checklists under production pressure

**FACT:** Haynes et al. (2009, *NEJM, 360*(5), 491–499, doi:10.1056/NEJMsa0810119) — WHO Surgical Safety Checklist across eight hospitals; death 1.5% → 0.8%, complications 11.0% → 7.0%; checklist is explicitly **time-feasible** at three junctures, not an infinite briefing.

**FACT:** Degani & Wiener (1990, NASA CR-177549) — production pressures (“making schedules”) interact with checklist misuse/non-use; design must respect interruption and usable length.

**FACT:** FAA AC **120-51E** lists effective **briefings** among CRM markers: coordination, planning, open communication, expectations for SOP deviations — briefs are **error-management before the event** (SAFE-AAR lineage).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Micro-brief is a **timeout checklist**, not a pre-flight lecture. If late join burns warm-up, **hold and resume** at the named edge — do not abandon the list. Exam-week schedule pressure predicts skip; product must make the short form the path of least resistance.

**Kill:** “Full CRM brief or nothing” purity that becomes skip; aviation costume ads.  
**Survive:** Cap-visible, interruption-safe four-beat open on the protect list.

## CXXIX.6 SHARP’s first beat is the brief — Set before How

**FACT:** Ahmed, Arora, Russ, Darzi, Vincent, & Sevdalis (2013, *Annals of Surgery, 258*(6), 958–963, doi:10.1097/SLA.0b013e31828c88fc) — **SHARP** scaffold for time-feasible OR debrief: **Set learning objectives**; How did it go; Address concerns; Review learning points; Plan ahead. Prospective pre/post 100 cases: OSAD medians 19 → 33 (*P* < 0.001). Companion positioning: SHARP as “absolute basics” when full OSAD-depth is impractical.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** SAFE-MICRODEB already borrows SHARP for the close. SAFE-MICROBRIEF owns the **Set** beat at the *open* — so the later How/Address/Review/Plan have something to compare against. A micro-debrief without a prior micro-brief collapses into vibes (“how did that feel?”) because expected was never named.

**Kill:** Close-only AAR theater without open aim.  
**Survive:** Pair MICROBRIEF + MICRODEB as one capped cycle under late join / LOADSHED.

## CXXIX.7 Expectancy ethics still bind the 30-second open

**FACT / doctrine carry:** SAFE-EXPECTANCY (Part XLVI) — task-only language; no trait labels; tutor briefs must not smuggle “you’re behind…” fate talk. High-dosage tutoring toolkits emphasize consistent **framing & objective** after a light open — not icebreaker-as-product (NSSA session-structure guidance; practice note, not a MindCraft RCT).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Under late join, the temptation is either (a) skip aim or (b) shame-speed (“we’re late so grind harder”). Both fail. Micro-brief copy stays **task-referenced**: edge, criterion, trap, coach role. One belonging beat ≤10s is allowed (“glad you’re here”) then into Set — not an 8-minute weekend recap when 12 minutes remain.

**Kill:** Late-join shame open; endless rapport under crunch as “care.”  
**Survive:** Warm + specific; SAFE-ERRCLIMATE destake without soft standards.

## CXXIX.9 Doctrine — SAFE-MICROBRIEF (provisional)

1. **Cap the form, don’t cancel the aim** — under late join / crunch, run micro-brief; never “no time for goals” as default (LOADSHED protect).  
2. **Four beats minimum** — named edge → success criterion → one trap/FormatId note → coach role.  
3. **Student synthesizes** — ≥1 restatement before first problem (I-PASS receiver synthesis analogue).  
4. **Pair with micro-debrief** — Set at open enables How/Plan at close (SAFE-MICRODEB).  
5. **Shared-model method** — SBAR/I-PASS structure without hospital cosplay; Starmer: structure ≠ longer.  
6. **Specific > do-your-best** — Locke & Latham: name the edge (Locke & Latham, 2002).  
7. **Expectancy-safe** — task language only; no late-join shame (SAFE-EXPECTANCY).  
8. **Copy:** “Even if we start late, we still name the edge and what success looks like — in under a minute.” Never “aviation-grade briefs raise ACT” or Brief Score™.

**Confidence:** High — Locke/Latham goal specificity + Starmer duration-neutral structure gains + SBAR shared-model + WHO time-boxed checklist + Degani/Wiener production-pressure warning + SHARP Set beat as method sources. Medium — MindCraft-specific 30–60s completeness thresholds and late-join protect-list compliance (needs MICROBRIEF-*). High — skip/pour-open / Brief Score™ / cosplay ACT ads as commercially toxic under FEI + SAFE-AAR.

## CXI.1 Why this chapter exists

Edtech defaults: (1) “formative assessment” as a marketing badge, (2) exit tickets / thumbs as proof of pedagogy, (3) Formative Score™ as an engagement KPI, (4) recycling Black & Wiliam’s popularized 0.4–0.7 effect-size legend as a product guarantee, (5) confusing *more quizzes* with *cycles that change instruction*. The research spine is sharper: formative assessment is a **process** of using evidence to improve learning; effects are real but **heterogeneous** and often smaller than legend; domain-specific design and teacher skill matter; feedback that does not cause a next action is “dangling data.”

MindCraft already owns pieces of the stack (soft-wrong, Map diagnosis, AAR-style debrief, FEI metrics). The open commercial question is whether to sell AfL cosplay or to operationalize **minute-by-minute cycles** that tutors and product can run — and falsify.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Becoming a mathematical thinker includes becoming someone whose *next attempt* is shaped by evidence — not someone who completes exit tickets for points.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:** Formative Score™ / Assessment Minutes NS; exit-ticket completion ≡ learning; Black–Wiliam 0.4–0.7 as ACT guarantee; more quizzes ≡ formative; gradebook cosplay; “got it?” ≡ cycle closed; AfL ads without domain-specific evidence use.

## CXI.2 Constructs (keep cycle ≠ quiz theater)

| Construct | Research meaning | MindCraft analogue | Failure mode |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Formative assessment** | Evidence elicited, interpreted, used to adjust teaching/learning | Session cycle: probe → soft-wrong/Map → next attempt | Quiz volume theater |
| **Summative** | Judgment of achievement for reporting | ACT practice tests; parent proof artifacts | Confusing live grades with FEI |
| **Exit ticket** | End-of-lesson evidence sample | Optional 1-item wrap tied to next mission | Ticket completion NS |
| **Learning intentions / success criteria** | Shared destination + quality standard | Mission target concept + “what correct looks like” | Vague “do your best” |
| **Feedback that moves forward** | Causes thinking + next action | Soft-wrong → retry_120s / challenge_accept | Praise / dump without action |
| **SAFE-FA** | Cycle fidelity under FEI, not AfL branding | This chapter | Formative Score™ ads |

**Operational definition (HYPOTHESIS):** A tutor session or Practice wrap is *SAFE-FA compliant* when (a) a learning intention is named at grain fine enough to falsify (concept/ingredient/FormatId — not “math”), (b) evidence is elicited *during* work (not only end-of-hour quizzes), (c) interpretation names a specific next move (retry, fade step, bridge hop, destake), (d) the student takes that next action in-session or within a short scheduled return, and (e) success is measured by FEI events (`retry_120s`, `challenge_accept`, delayed `solo_transfer_pass`) — never Formative Score™ or exit-ticket completion rate.

## CXI.3 Black & Wiliam — process claim, not a product effect-size SKU

**FACT (full review):** Black & Wiliam (1998a, *Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice, 5*(1), 7–74, doi:10.1080/0969595980050102) — comprehensive review of classroom formative assessment (~250 sources). Innovations strengthening frequent feedback can yield substantial gains; student self-assessment and teacher strategy matter. They **declined a single amalgamation meta-analysis** because study differences made pooling misleading — a caution often erased in marketing retellings.

**FACT (popular summary):** Black & Wiliam (1998b, *Phi Delta Kappan, 80*(2), 139–148) — *Inside the Black Box* popularizes effect-size language often quoted as **0.4–0.7**. This is the citation path most edtech decks abuse.

**FACT (definitional spine):** Wiliam (2011, *Embedded Formative Assessment*, Solution Tree) — formative assessment uses evidence of achievement to adjust next instructional steps so those steps are better than they would have been without the evidence; five strategies: clarify intentions/success criteria; elicit evidence; feedback that moves learning forward; peers as resources; students as owners of learning.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Cite Black & Wiliam for the **process** (evidence → adjust → close gap), never as “+0.5σ from exit tickets.” MindCraft map: intentions = mission target + success criteria; elicit = attempt + soft-wrong + Map probes; feedback = SAFE-FBTIME; peers = SAFE-PEERX when allowed; ownership = SAFE-SRL / FEI.

**Kill:** 0.4–0.7 as product guarantee / ACT points from “we do formative assessment.”  
**Survive:** Formative-as-process; five strategies as playbook checklist, not a Formative Score™.

## CXI.4 Sadler — feedback must close a gap, or it is dangling data

**FACT:** Sadler (1989, *Instructional Science, 18*(2), 119–144, doi:10.1007/BF00117714) — formative assessment theory for qualitative judgments: feedback is only formative when the learner (1) understands the standard, (2) compares current performance to that standard, and (3) takes action to close the gap. Without evaluative skill and tactics, teacher comments become **dangling data**. Goal of instructional systems: transition from external feedback toward learner self-monitoring.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Tutor talk that ends at “good effort” or AI monologue that never forces a next attempt fails Sadler’s third condition. Soft-wrong + required retry densifies Sadler; unlimited dump fails it. Exit tickets that never change the next mission are dangling data with a timestamp.

**Kill:** Ticket-without-action; praise fog ≡ formative.  
**Survive:** Standard + comparison + next move as the cycle unit.

## CXI.5 Effect-size honesty — legend vs Kingston–Nash vs uncertainty

**FACT (meta wound on legend):** Kingston & Nash (2011, *Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice, 30*(4), 28–37, doi:10.1111/j.1745-3992.2011.00220.x) — reviewed 300+ K–12 formative-assessment efficacy studies; only 13 yielded usable effect sizes (42 independent ES). Median ES **0.25**; random-effects mean **0.20**. Moderators: ELA ≈0.32, mathematics ≈0.17, science ≈0.09; PD-based and computer-based implementations ≈0.30 / 0.28. Explicitly challenges the oft-claimed 0.40–0.70 range.

**FACT (meta commentary / further uncertainty):** Briggs, Ruiz-Primo, Furtak, Shepard, & Yin (2012, *Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice, 31*(4), 56–63, doi:10.1111/j.1745-3992.2012.00252.x) — methodological critique of Kingston & Nash: inclusion decisions and outcome measures leave **considerable uncertainty** about the true average effect; do not treat 0.20 as the new dogma either. (Note: Black & Wiliam themselves warned against amalgamation.)

**FACT (definition / claim critique):** Bennett (2011, *Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice, 18*(1), 5–25, doi:10.1080/0969594X.2010.513678) — “formative assessment” is not yet a well-defined set of artefacts; quantitative effectiveness claims are often suspect (untraceable/flawed/dated sources); domain specificity, measurement principles, and teacher support are under-specified; maximum benefit requires domain-rooted designs inside a coherent system.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Commercial honesty: formative cycles can help; **math effects are not magic**; MindCraft must specify *what evidence* and *what adjustment* in the ontology/FEI system — Bennett’s domain point is a gift to the knowledge-graph lane, not a license for AfL cosplay.

**Kill:** Formative Score™; “AfL raises achievement 0.7σ” ads; quiz-count NS.  
**Survive:** Modest, heterogeneous, domain-dependent gains → design for *specified cycles*, instrument FEI, pre-register FA-*.

## CXI.6 Feedback power is real — and level-conditional (bridge to SAFE-FBTIME)

**FACT:** Hattie & Timperley (2007, *Review of Educational Research, 77*(1), 81–112, doi:10.3102/003465430298487) — feedback among powerful influences but highly variable; effective feedback answers where going / how going / where next; levels: task, process, self-regulation, self — **self/praise** often weak or harmful relative to task/process/SR.

**FACT (updated meta):** Wisniewski, Zierer, & Hattie (2020, *Frontiers in Psychology, 10*, 3087, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087) — 435 studies, k=994: overall feedback effect on learning **d ≈ 0.48** with large heterogeneity; information content matters; cognitive/motor outcomes > motivational/behavioral on average.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Session cycles should prefer task/process/SR feedback that schedules a next attempt (SAFE-FBTIME), not ego toasts. Densifies SAFE-EXPLAIN / SAFE-HINT: short, actionable, contingent.

**Kill:** Praise-as-formative; longer≡better feedback essays.  
**Survive:** Where-to-next feedback as the third Sadler condition in product language.

## CXI.7 Exit tickets vs FEI — when a wrap helps, when it is theater

**HYPOTHESIS (product law):** Exit tickets are allowed only as **evidence samples that change the next move** (Map route, mission level, destake, FormatId hop). They are banned as:
1. Completion NS / XP for ticket submit.  
2. Substitute for in-session elicitation (ticket-only “formative”).  
3. Parent-facing gradebook cosplay.  
4. Calm/mood Formative Score™.

**Contrast with FEI North Stars (SAFE-INSTRUMENT):** `retry_120s`, `challenge_accept`, `transfer_pass`, `solo_transfer_pass` already measure whether evidence changed behavior. A ticket that never predicts those events is dangling data.

**Kill:** Exit-ticket completion as learning KPI.  
**Survive:** Optional 1-item wrap **wired** to next Practice path + tutor brief.

## CXI.8 Tutor session spine — Black/Wiliam grain without classroom cosplay

**HYPOTHESIS (HITL playbook):** A 45-min session (SAFE-DP) runs SAFE-FA as:

1. **Intention (2 min):** Name concept/FormatId + success criterion (“solo retry without hard peek”).  
2. **Elicit (ongoing):** Student attempts first; probes diagnose misconception/bridge/format (SAFE-MISCON / SAFE-BRIDGE / SAFE-FORMAT).  
3. **Interpret (spoken aloud briefly):** One specific bottleneck — not a lecture.  
4. **Adjust + close gap (bulk of session):** Contingent hint/fade; required retry; delayed transfer probe if time.  
5. **Ownership handoff (3–5 min):** Student states next move; product schedules return (SAFE-SRL / SAFE-AAR light).

**Kill:** Explain-first brand; “got it?” as closed cycle; talk-% as formative proof (SAFE-TALK).  
**Survive:** Evidence-use fidelity checklist for QA (SAFE-HITL).

## CXI.13 Doctrine — SAFE-FA (provisional)

1. **Process, not badge** — formative = evidence used to improve next steps (Black & Wiliam; Wiliam 2011).  
2. **No legend σ** — ban 0.4–0.7 / “AfL raises ACT” marketing (Kingston & Nash; Bennett; Briggs et al.).  
3. **Sadler completeness** — standard + compare + act; dangling data fails (Sadler 1989).  
4. **Domain-specific cycles** — ontology/FormatId/bridge grain, not generic AfL (Bennett).  
5. **Feedback that moves** — task/process/SR + where-next; densify SAFE-FBTIME/EXPLAIN/HINT (Hattie & Timperley; Wisniewski et al.).  
6. **Exit tickets are optional wires** — never completion NS; must change next mission.  
7. **FEI over Formative Score™** — instrument retry/challenge/transfer, not Assessment Minutes.  
8. **HITL fidelity** — tutor QA on cycle steps; ban explain-first and “got it?” theater (SAFE-HITL/TALK).  
9. **No Formative Score™ / Assessment Minutes NS / ticket≡learning / 0.4–0.7 ads / quiz-volume≡formative / gradebook cosplay / AfL ACT guarantees**.

**Confidence:** High that formative-as-process and Sadler’s gap-closing conditions are the right commercial spine, and that popularized 0.4–0.7 claims are unsafe. Medium that the SAFE-FA session spine will beat explain-first on delayed FEI transfer — instrument FA-* before AfL marketing. High confidence to kill Formative Score™, exit-ticket completion NS, and effect-size legend ads regardless.

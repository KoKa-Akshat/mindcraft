## 1. Creative direction (read this before the script)

### Tone
Calm Twin Cities confidence. A founder who has sat across the table from Maya for eight years, not a founder who read about her. Short declaratives, spoken pauses, no hype inflection at the end of sentences. The room should feel like Akshat is telling them something true he has watched happen many times, not performing a pitch he memorized. Certain, human, unflinching, per the Brand Book personality dials, translated to a live investor-adjacent room rather than in-product copy.

### Emotional arc (six beats, do not reorder)
1. **Hook** (0:00): recognition. The room hears a sentence about themselves or someone they love.
2. **Human truth** (0:20): the wound, named plainly. No pity, no cartoon villain, just a broken hour that repeats every week in this room's own zip codes.
3. **Mechanism** (1:00): relief. "Oh, that's clever": answers are free now, diagnosis isn't, and MindCraft sells clarity to the person who's actually paying.
4. **Product proof** (1:45): credibility. Three real screens, pointed at, not narrated pixel by pixel. This is live, not a mockup.
5. **Future that the product already seeds** (2:55): lift. One controlled paragraph of the world-as-math vision, landed and then immediately tied back to tonight's screens so nobody thinks it's vaporware.
6. **Ask** (3:35): clarity. Exactly three kinds of people to introduce, one email, done.

### What must stick
- **One mechanism:** gap scan, then a college tutor who starts on the exact gap, never page one.
- **One future:** the same map is the seed of math as a world with a story, proven questions, and a tutor who knows it.
- **One ask:** an Edina/Wayzata/St. Paul ACT parent, a counselor, or a Twin Cities college tutor. Say "yes" on the form.

### What to cut, on sight
- Any origin-story detail beyond one sentence of founder credibility. The full founder story lives on LinkedIn (see §8); Demo Night is not the place to retell it.
- Roblox, Horizon 3 procedural generation, or any multi-sentence detour into the far future. One paragraph of vision, then back to the product.
- Narrating every pixel of a screenshot. Point, name the one thing that matters, move on.
- Any sentence that could be mistaken for a chatbot demo, a marketplace pitch, or a score guarantee. If a line could appear in a Khanmigo or Chegg deck, cut it.
- Apologetic hedging. The Brand Book's rule holds on stage too: MindCraft speaks in declaratives because the engine actually knows things.

### The single hardest sentence in the room
Someone will wonder, silently or out loud, "is this just AI doing the tutoring?" The script answers this before it's asked (see mechanism beat and Q&A #2). Never let that question go unanswered by the time the ask lands, or the feedback form's "what to sharpen" field will say "confused about the AI part."

## 2. Guidelines for slide writers and image models

Hand this section, plus the asset zip, to whoever builds the deck (ChatGPT or a designer). Fable 5 owns words and direction; this is the translation layer.

### Palette
Match the **live marketing site**, not the in-app dark cinematic system. Demo Night is a marketing-site moment (parents, counselors, mentors in the room), so the deck should feel like `mindcraft-marketing-site.web.app` walked on stage:
- Forest ink `#143a2e` (text, structure)
- Cream `#fffdf7` (ground, paper)
- Lime `#c4f547` (the one accent that means "this just changed," used once per slide, never as decoration)
- Deep field `#0c1a14` (only for the cold-open slide and the vision slide, to mark the two moments that are allowed to feel cinematic instead of paper-lit)

Do not import the app's Deep Field / Chalk / Stakes-red system wholesale. That system is for the product experience students live inside. This deck is the front door, so it wears the front door's colors.

### Typography vibe
- **Wordmark ("Mind*Craft*"):** Fredoka, gold or lime emphasis on "Craft," exactly as on the live site.
- **Slide headlines and product/mechanism slides:** a clean geometric sans (Nunito Sans / Inter Tight family), huge, one loud statement per slide.
- **The vision slide only:** switch to an editorial serif (DM Serif Display, matching the site's `hero-caption` treatment) for the headline and subline. This one typographic shift *is* the Katha-versus-engine distinction from the Brand Book, played on a single slide: the system speaks in sans, the story speaks in serif, and the room feels the register change without being told why.
- Never: rounded "friendly" fonts, handwriting fonts, chalkboard textures, comic-adjacent anything.

### One idea per slide
Every slide earns exactly one sentence of headline (≤8 words) and one supporting line. If a slide needs two ideas, it is two slides. See the Slide Bible in §5 for the enforced version.

### Imagery
- Real product screenshots only, from `asset_pack/screens/` (gap scan, map, tutor view). Do not fake UI, do not invent a screen that doesn't exist yet.
- Slide 2 (Gillian's quote) and slide 7 (Sebastian's quote) pull real text and the real photo/video still directly from `index.html`'s live testimonial section (`img/testimonials/sebastian-feedback.mp4` has a poster frame usable as a static image if video is impractical on stage). Do not paraphrase either quote; use the exact wording.
- The knowledge graph / map art is the one visual that is allowed to feel beautiful and abstract (constellation on dark ground, per Brand Book §9). Use it on the vision slide.
- No stock photography of smiling students. No mascots, chat bubbles, trophies, confetti, or badge-cabinet iconography, on any slide, ever.
- Founder photos: plain, real, on cream ground. Not a "meet the team" grid with headshots and job titles in Comic Sans energy; keep it as understated as the product screenshots.

### Citation rules
Real human proof now carries the weight academic citations were holding: Gillian's and Sebastian's actual words on slides 2 and 7 (both already live on the marketing site, both real, both named with consent) are stronger evidence than a study citation, and the brief itself says to prefer human proof over score guarantees. Zero academic citations remain in the slide bible. If a future version of this deck needs one (e.g., a skeptical investor audience asking "does tutoring even work"), style it exactly like the live site's `hero-cite`: small, link-styled, underlined, easy to skip, never more than one line.

### Hard gates (apply to every slide, no exceptions)
No em dashes. No "Bloom 2-sigma." No "AI replaces tutors." No "learn anything with anyone" marketplace framing. No confetti, no "you're brilliant," no grit-poster typography. No claim above L0 (what ships today) or L1 (mechanism, honestly described) unless a cited path proves higher.

## 6. Q&A bank (8 answers, prepped for whichever 2 land)

**1. How is MindCraft different from Khan Academy, Duolingo, or a tutoring marketplace?**
They sell a library, a habit loop, or access to hours. We sell the map before the hour: the exact concept and misconception a tutor should open with, so the human part of tutoring, the forty minutes with an actual person, stops being spent guessing.

**2. Is this an AI chatbot tutor?**
No. AI helps diagnose and prep the next question; a human tutor still does the teaching and builds the trust. We don't let AI hand over full solutions before a student has genuinely tried; the whole design is built so tutoring gets better, not replaced.

**3. What's your traction right now?**
We're live with Twin Cities families today, and we're honest that it's early. Our focus this quarter is design partners we can point to by name, not vanity numbers.

**4. Why start with ACT Math instead of everything?**
Most startups that die, die because they never found a real market. We picked one on purpose: Twin Cities juniors and seniors staring down the ACT, and the college tutors already teaching them. Solve that completely before we touch anything else.

**5. How do you make money?**
Subscription from families, priced for the value of not restarting from zero every single session. We're not discounting early just to look busy; school and B2B partnerships come later, once the family side is proven.

**6. Isn't "math as a world" just another big ed-tech vision?**
Every part of that world runs on the same forty two concepts we ship today. It's not a slide, it's the actual spine underneath the gap scan you just saw; we're building the floor before we build the house.

**7. How do you find and vet your tutors?**
Right now, personally, mostly current students and recent grads from Macalester, St. Olaf, and St. Thomas, people who remember their own gap firsthand. As we grow, the bar stays the same: someone we'd want teaching a sibling.

**8. What about student data and privacy?**
We store what a tutor actually needs to teach well: where the gap is and what worked, nothing beyond that. Parents can see it too; it's built to be looked at, not hidden.

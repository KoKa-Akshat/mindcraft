## CXXXVI.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-XFERGRAIN settled the *path*: one-tap, edge-matched, short Practice grain after instrumental help. It left the *force* underspecified. Ops and growth instincts split:

1. **Optional chip** — “Practice when ready” footer → dump-and-quit returns (hostile default).  
2. **Soft-require** — primary “Prove this alone (1–3)” with demoted Close / “Not now — schedule return.”  
3. **Hard-gate** — modal wall; dismiss disabled until grain starts (or completes).  
4. **Marathon hard-gate** — cannot leave until 10 solo items (already killed by XFERGRAIN).

Hard-gate maximizes short-run `followon_start`. It also risks **reactance** (freedom threat → boomerang: skip Solver next time, binge executive help elsewhere, or quit). Soft-require preserves autonomy language while keeping the pedagogical default honest. Help-avoidance research warns: students who already feel threatened by asking for help will treat a forced “prove you deserved the hint” wall as competence shame — the opposite of SAFE-HELP’s invite path.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Instrumental help without a transfer grain is unfinished pedagogy — but unfinished pedagogy fixed by *controlling* walls trains Maya to avoid the help door. Soft-require is the fiduciary default; hard-gate is a clinical escalation with kill switches.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. Hard-gate wall as universal default after every soft hint.  
2. Optional buried chip as the only “respectful” close (dump-and-quit cosplay).  
3. Gate Score™ / Forced Follow-On % / Soft-Require Compliance Minutes as North Star.  
4. “No Close until you prove” shame copy after help.  
5. Ignoring help-avoidance / Solver-skip as falsifiers when `followon_start` rises.  
6. Marathon hard-gates (already killed).  
7. ACT / retention guarantees from soft- vs hard-gate packaging.

## CXXXVI.3 Soft-require is a nudge; hard-gate is a mandate

**FACT:** Thaler & Sunstein (2008, *Nudge*, Yale University Press) — a nudge alters behavior in a predictable way **without forbidding options** or significantly changing economic incentives; “Nudges are not mandates. Putting the fruit at eye level counts as a nudge. Banning junk food does not.”

**FACT:** Johnson & Goldstein (2003, *Science, 302*(5649), 1338–1339, doi:10.1126/science.1091721) — defaults dominate outcomes; opt-out architectures raise uptake vs opt-in without removing choice (already used in SAFE-CRUNCHCTA / SAFE-XFERGRAIN).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Soft-require = fruit at eye level (primary CTA) with junk food still on the shelf (Close). Hard-gate = ban. MindCraft may *sometimes* need a mandate for fiduciary reasons (Bastani unassisted harm), but calling every mandate a “nudge” is brand fraud. Default soft-require inherits Johnson/Goldstein power *without* Brehm-level freedom elimination.

**Kill:** Marketing hard-gates as “gentle nudges.”  
**Survive:** Soft-require as default choice architecture.  
**Wound:** Organ-donation defaults ≠ teen homework psychology — measure quit/help-avoid in product, not cite consent rates as FEI.

## CXXXVI.4 Controlling language and hard walls arouse reactance

**FACT:** Brehm (1966, *A Theory of Psychological Reactance*, Academic Press) — threats to or elimination of valued free behaviors arouse reactance, a motivational state to restore freedom (boomerang, source derogation, opposite behavior).

**FACT:** Ball & Goodboy (2014, *Communication Education, 63*(3), 192–209, doi:10.1080/03634523.2014.918634) — college classroom experiment (N=206): instructor messages using **forceful** (and/or unclear) language raised perceived freedom threat → psychological reactance → intentions toward instructional dissent and challenge behavior.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** “You must complete Practice before closing Solver” is forceful language + freedom elimination. Expected restoration moves in product: rage-quit, never open Solver again, paste ChatGPT off-platform (executive help displacement), or speed-click the grain without sense-making (try-step abuse — SAFE-HELP). Soft-require with rationale (“same edge, one short proof so help stays instrumental”) lowers threat while keeping the pedagogical cue.

**Kill:** Controlling “can’t leave” copy as default.  
**Survive:** Clear, autonomy-framed soft-require; hard-gate only with measured reactance falsifiers.  
**Wound:** College communication lab ≠ ACT HS product — SOFTGATE-* must instrument Solver-skip and off-platform help proxies where possible.

## CXXXVI.5 Autonomy support beats controlling pressure for quality motivation

**FACT:** Deci & Ryan (2000, *Psychological Inquiry, 11*(4), 227–268, doi:10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01) — Self-Determination Theory: social contexts that support autonomy, competence, and relatedness facilitate internalization and better quality motivation; controlling contexts that pressure toward specific outcomes undermine intrinsic motivation and forestall integration.

**FACT (already company-adjacent, SAFE-REWARD / XLIV):** Controlling rewards and pressure can crowd out autonomy — relevant when “forced Practice minutes” become the payment for having received a hint.

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable):** Framing the grain as *Maya’s chance to own the win* (competence + autonomy) beats framing it as *the tax for getting help* (controlling contingency). Hard-gate that feels like a fine for asking will raise help-avoidance among students who already see help as costly (next section). Soft-require + SCHED “Not now → return chip” preserves relatedness to the Desk without pretending dump-and-quit is fine.

**Kill:** “Hint tax” / punishment framing for follow-on.  
**Survive:** Autonomy-supportive proof framing + competence evidence (SAFE-PROOF).  
**Wound:** SDT is broad; do not claim soft-require raises ACT points — claim better help-recruit + solo co-primary balance.

## CXXXVI.6 Help-avoidance is the commercial falsifier hard-gates often ignore

**FACT:** Ryan & Pintrich (1997, *Journal of Educational Psychology, 89*(2), 329–341, doi:10.1037/0022-0663.89.2.329) — early adolescents in math (N=203): perceived **threats** and **benefits** of help-seeking shape avoidance; threats/benefits partially mediate goal and competence effects on avoidance; only benefits predicted adaptive help-seeking. Students unsure of competence/social standing feel more threatened when asking.

**FACT:** Karabenick & Knapp (1991, *Journal of Educational Psychology, 83*(2), 221–230, doi:10.1037/0022-0663.83.2.221) — academic help-seeking relates to other instrumental achievement behaviors; avoidance and executive patterns are distinct from instrumental seeking (spine already in SAFE-HELP).

**FACT:** Aleven et al. (2016, *IJAIED, 26*(1), 205–223, doi:10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1) — help helps, but only so much; learners both abuse and avoid help; design that only punishes misuse can worsen under-use.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** If hard-gate raises `followon_start` while **`solver_open_rate` falls** or students report “I don’t ask because then I’m trapped in Practice,” the gate failed SAFE-HELP. Commercial identity companies cannot optimize forced proof by shrinking the help door. Soft-require + binge detectors (abuse side) + help-invite (avoidance side) is the balanced stack; hard-gate is a scalpel for chronic dumpers who still *open* Solver, not a default for anxious avoiders.

**Kill:** Follow-on compliance as sole success when help-recruit collapses.  
**Survive:** Co-primary `followon_start` × help/Solver openness.  
**Wound:** Survey threat ≠ product telemetry — combine SOFTGATE-QUAL with event rates.

## CXXXVI.7 Defaults work — but defaults ≠ outcomes; don’t worship Forced %

**FACT:** Research distinguishing default effects on *choices* vs *downstream outcomes* (e.g. defaults raising registration without guaranteeing later completion; see behavioural-public-policy discussions such as default effects on exam registration vs exam-taking) — uptake of an offered path is not durable learning.

**Already company law:** Bastani et al. (2025, *PNAS*, doi:10.1073/pnas.2422633122) — assisted practice fluency ≠ unassisted exam learning. SAFE-XFERGRAIN requires solo grain *quality*, not merely modal compliance.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Hard-gate can inflate `followon_start` with rage-clicks and empty attempts. Soft-require may yield fewer starts but cleaner `xfer_near_pass` / `retry_120s`. Prefer quality of solo evidence over Forced Follow-On %. If soft-require yields near-zero starts, escalate *friction on Close* (confirm + SCHED chip) before full hard-gate — a middle rung.

**Kill:** Gate Score™ / Forced % as learning NS.  
**Survive:** Soft-require → confirm-on-close → conditional hard-gate ladder.  
**Wound:** Need SOFTGATE-1…3 before declaring ladder universal.

## CXXXVI.8 Product rules (SAFE-SOFTGATE)

1. **Default = soft-require** — Primary “Prove this alone (1–3)”; demote Close / “Not now.”  
2. **Never optional-only chip** as the sole post-help affordance (dump-and-quit).  
3. **Autonomy copy** — “Ready to prove the same edge alone?” Never “You can’t leave until you Practice.”  
4. **Not-now path** — Always pairs with SAFE-SCHED return chip (honest deferral ≠ dump).  
5. **Hard-gate is conditional** — Trigger only on repeated dump-and-quit *and* healthy Solver/help openness; never day-one universal hard-gate.  
6. **Hard-gate grain stays short** — 1–3 items; start-gate preferred over finish-gate; ban marathon locks.  
7. **Falsifiers stop escalation** — Rising `help_avoid_after_gate`, falling `solver_open_rate`, QUAL “trapped after hint” → roll back to soft-require + confirm-on-close.  
8. **Abuse ≠ avoidance** — Keep binge/executive detectors (SAFE-HELP); do not “fix” avoidance with harder walls.  
9. **Telemetry** — `followon_start`, `xfer_near_pass`, `close_without_followon`, `help_avoid_after_gate`, `solver_open_rate`; demote Gate Score™ / Forced %.  
10. **Parent copy** — “Unstuck → invited to prove alone”; never shame “skipped proof” streaks on parent dash (SAFE-PDASH / SOFTMSG).

**Confidence:** High — Thaler/Sunstein nudge≠mandate + Brehm/Ball–Goodboy reactance + Deci/Ryan autonomy + Ryan/Pintrich help-threat + Aleven help-avoidance as method sources for soft-require default. Medium — exact hard-gate trigger policy and confirm-on-close middle rung (SOFTGATE-*). High — universal hard-gate / Gate Score™ / hint-tax shame / ignoring avoidance falsifiers as commercially toxic under FEI + SAFE-HELP/XFERGRAIN.

## CXXXVI.10 So what for MindCraft commercially

- **Copy:** “You got unstuck — ready to prove the same edge alone?” Secondary: “Not now — we’ll bring this edge back.” Never “No exit until Practice.”  
- **Product:** Soft-require default; confirm-on-close middle; conditional short hard-gate only with avoidance falsifiers green.  
- **Ops / analytics:** Dashboard `followon_start` *beside* `solver_open_rate` / `help_avoid_after_gate`; demote Forced %.  
- **Positioning:** Against Chegg-close *and* against compliance-app walls; high-reliability craft = proof invitation that does not punish asking.  
- **Metric:** Co-primary follow-on × help openness × solo pass — demote Gate Score™.  
- **Kill list:** Universal hard-gate; optional-only chip; Gate Score™; hint-tax shame; ignore avoidance when Forced % rises; ACT guarantees from gate packaging.  
- **Growth / vision:** Acquisition can sell unstuck; retention defends **willing** return to the help door. The Desk teaches Maya to recruit support *and* to prove alone — without making proof the fine for having asked.

## 1. Creative direction

### The audience should leave with three things

1. **The belief:** The student was never bad at math. The story lost them, and the system kept moving.
2. **The mechanism:** A gap scan becomes a living map so the tutor starts at the real gap.
3. **The action:** Introduce MindCraft to an ACT family, a trusted counselor, or a Minnesota college tutor.

### The emotional arc

**Recognition**

A parent has seen the defeated sentence: "I am just not a math person."

**Relief**

MindCraft does not treat the student as the problem. It locates the break.

**Trust**

The product does not replace the tutor. It makes the human hour more precise and gives learning continuity.

**Wonder**

The same concept map can become a world where math has purpose, consequence, and story.

**Action**

The room knows exactly who to introduce.

### The strategic staircase

| Horizon | Honest stage language | Product state |
|---|---|---|
| Live now | Gap scan, concept map, tutor discovery, story-wrapped lessons | Show real screens |
| Building next | Tutor brief, session memory, parent signal: clicked, stuck, next | Describe as the continuity layer |
| Long vision | The map becomes a living world shaped by concepts and interests | Show as the future seeded by today's spine |

### The central creative choice

Do not pitch a sequence of features. Pitch the recovery of a student's story.

The student says, "I am bad at math."

MindCraft answers, "No. You are here."

The map identifies the place. The tutor helps the student cross it. The system remembers the crossing. In time, the map becomes a world worth exploring.

### Tone

- Calm Twin Cities confidence
- Founder-lived, not category-theorized
- Specific enough for a parent to trust
- Ambitious enough for a builder or investor to lean forward
- Short declarative sentences
- No score guarantees
- No generic AI language
- No em dashes

## 2. Four-minute stage script

**Target:** 530 to 540 words at a measured 132 to 136 words per minute  
**Format:** Akshat opens and closes. Blake owns one clean product bridge.

### Slide 1: You were never bad at math.

**AKSHAT**

They said American kids are bad at math.

I disagree.

For eight years, from Macalester classrooms to one-on-one tutoring, I kept meeting students who had already decided math was not for them.

Usually, they were not missing all of math. Somewhere, one idea stopped making sense. The class kept moving. The student turned that moment into an identity.

### Slide 2: The hour starts too late.

Then a parent hires a tutor.

The tutor is smart. The student is trying. But the first half of the hour becomes detective work.

Was it fractions? Algebra? Slope? Confidence? A problem from three years ago that nobody noticed?

By the time the tutor finds the real break, the hour is almost gone. Next week, everyone starts over.

That is the problem we built MindCraft to solve.

### Slide 3: MindCraft is the memory.

MindCraft is the memory between the student and the tutor.

The student takes a gap scan. MindCraft turns the result into a living map of what is solid, what is shaky, and where to begin.

The tutor does not start at page one. The tutor starts at the gap.

### Slide 4: Map the gap.

**BLAKE**

The live product already has the first pieces.

A gap scan across ACT Math. A concept map that shows the route. Story-wrapped practice. A way to find a college tutor who can meet the student there.

We are connecting those pieces into a Living Learning Record: what the student tried, where they got stuck, what finally clicked, and what should happen next.

The data is useful because a human uses it.

### Slide 5: The tutor starts there.

**AKSHAT**

That last sentence matters.

We are not building an AI replacement for tutors. Answers are abundant. A trusted person who can read the room, choose the right analogy, and stay with a student through hard work is still scarce.

MindCraft handles the continuity around that relationship, so the human hour can stay human.

### Slide 6: Parents get clarity.

The student uses MindCraft. The parent buys it.

Parents do not want another job as the math teacher. They want to know three things:

What clicked? Where are they stuck? What happens next?

We are starting with ACT Math for Twin Cities families because the need is urgent, the buyer is clear, and the outcome matters now.

### Slide 7: The map becomes a world.

But ACT Math is the front door, not the edge of the idea.

Imagine the same 42-concept map becoming a world.

Trigonometry is a boat holding its bearing against a current. Algebra is a studio where the sound only works when the equation does. Geometry is a structure that stands because the student understood why.

The fog lifts as mastery grows.

### Slide 8: Today is the first map.

That future does not begin someday.

The gap scan is the first map. The college tutor is the first human guide. The story lessons are the first places in the world. The Living Learning Record is how the journey remembers.

We start narrow enough to earn trust, then build toward the reason we started.

Every student deserves the moment when math clicks and they realize:

I was never bad at this.

### Slide 9: Help us find the first families.

If someone comes to mind tonight, put their name in the form:

A parent of a 10th or 11th grader preparing for ACT Math.

A counselor or coach trusted by Twin Cities families.

A Minnesota college student who could become an extraordinary tutor.

We are MindCraft.

ACT? We got you.

## 7. Q&A bank

### Is MindCraft an AI tutor?

No. MindCraft is the continuity around a human tutoring relationship. The product maps the gap, preserves useful context, and guides targeted practice. The tutor still does the work that requires judgment, trust, and a real relationship.

### What is live today?

The live product includes the gap scan, concept map, story-wrapped practice, and tutor discovery. The next layer is the Living Learning Record, including tutor context and a simple parent signal.

### Why ACT Math first?

It gives us a clear student, buyer, deadline, and concept set. Twin Cities families already spend money trying to solve this problem. A focused front door lets us earn trust and measure whether the mechanism works.

### Why start with parents instead of schools?

The student uses the product, but the parent makes the first purchase decision. Parents can move faster, feel the urgency directly, and tell us whether clarity and better use of tutoring time are valuable. Schools can be a later channel, not the first dependency.

### How is this different from Khan, Brilliant, ChatGPT, or a tutoring marketplace?

Khan wins on content coverage. Brilliant wins on interactive delight. ChatGPT wins on instant answers. Marketplaces provide access to tutors. MindCraft connects diagnosis, a living concept map, a human tutor, and continuity between sessions for the student who already feels lost.

### What evidence do you have that it improves ACT scores?

We do not make a score guarantee today. This beta is designed to measure the steps that should precede one: whether we locate useful gaps, reduce tutor detective time, produce independent transfer after help, and give parents a clearer next step. Score movement becomes a measured claim only after the data earns it.

### Why college tutors?

They can be credible near-peer guides for high school students and strong local partners for families. MindCraft gives them better context so more of the hour can go to the human part of tutoring. We are building a trusted local network, not an open global marketplace.

### How do you maintain tutor quality?

Start narrow. Recruit carefully from Minnesota colleges, observe early sessions, give every tutor the same concept map and context format, and use transfer checks to see whether an intervention carried. The beta should teach us what a repeatable quality standard requires.

### What is the business model?

The first model is parent-paid ACT support with tutoring and product continuity. Exact packaging and price should be tested with beta families. We will price around the value of clearer diagnosis and better use of paid tutoring time, not around generic app minutes.

### Is the world vision a distraction from test prep?

It would be if it used a different product spine. It does not. The ACT gap scan, the 42-concept map, story-wrapped practice, and the human guide are the first version of the same world. We are proving the map in an urgent market before expanding what the map can become.

### Why will a student use it?

Because the experience is designed for the student who goes quiet in evaluative math settings. The first promise is not "do more practice." It is "we can show you where you are, give today's work a reason, and help you prove the skill when the hints are gone." Whether that drives return is one of the beta's core tests.

### What are you asking from this room?

Named introductions. We want parents of 10th and 11th graders preparing for ACT Math, counselors and coaches trusted by those families, and Minnesota college students who could become excellent tutors.

## 12. Final editorial judgment

The pitch should not end at "we save tutor time." That is useful, but too small.

It should not begin with the world either. That is beautiful, but too ungrounded.

The right climb is:

> A student internalized a lost moment.  
> A paid tutoring hour wastes time finding it.  
> MindCraft maps it.  
> A human tutor starts there.  
> The system remembers what works.  
> The same map becomes a world.  
> Tonight, help us find the first families.

That is one company at three horizons.

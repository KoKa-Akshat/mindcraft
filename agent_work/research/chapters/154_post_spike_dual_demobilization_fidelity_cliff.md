## CLIV.1 Why this chapter exists

SURGEPANEL chose make-or-buy under spike. PANELRAMP gated authority *in*. The missing half:

## CLIV.3 Flexible staffing exists to match load — demobilization is the point, not a failure

**FACT:** Houseman (2001, *Industrial and Labor Relations Review, 55*(1), 149–170, doi:10.1177/001979390105500109) — nationally representative U.S. establishment survey: employers widely use agency temporaries, short-term hires, on-call, part-time, and contract workers; the **most commonly cited reasons** across flexible arrangements were needs to **adjust for workload fluctuations** and **cover absences**; many also use temps/part-timers to screen for regular jobs; benefit-cost savings matter for some arrangements.

**Reuse (SURGEPANEL):** Davis-Blake & Uzzi (1993, *Administrative Science Quarterly, 38*(2), 195–223, doi:10.2307/2393411) — **variation in employment needs** positively predicts temporary-worker use; **firm-specific training / high informational-technical skill** negatively predict temp use.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Exam-week dual overflow is classic fluctuation use. Demobilization when λ falls is **success of the employment mode**, not an ops embarrassment. Failure modes are (a) treating demob as unplanned crisis, (b) inventing load to avoid ending the contract, (c) leaving firm-specific seal work on forever-temps after the fluctuation ends (Davis-Blake–Uzzi wound).

**Kill:** Forever-panel as default “care.” **Survive:** Fluctuation → contract → planned exit. **Wound:** Houseman’s benefit-cost motives ≠ MindCraft seal ethics — never demob to dodge training obligations mid-ramp (PANELRAMP funds ramp).

## CLIV.5 Blended workforces have social costs — demob can heal or deepen the wound

**FACT:** Broschak & Davis-Blake (2006, *Academy of Management Journal, 49*(2), 371–393, doi:10.5465/amj.2006.20786085) — higher nonstandard shares associate with worse supervisor/peer attitudes, higher turnover intentions, and less helping (contingent on mobility, arrangement type, contact).

**FACT:** Davis-Blake, Broschak, & George (2003, *Academy of Management Journal, 46*(4), 475–485, doi:10.5465/30040639) — blending can worsen manager–employee relations, cut loyalty, and raise exit/voice interest among standard employees (contingent on temp vs contract and role level).

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Cliff-cut that dumps panel backlog onto staff is a second blending shock. Name staff dual leads through handover; ban utilization shame (“panel idle = waste”); never let last-week panel authority outrank staff on Map bits.

**Kill:** Idle-shame → invent-work. **Survive:** Status rules through exit. **Wound:** Do not import unionization findings into product copy.

## CLIV.6 Contingent work moves knowledge — exit without capture is a fidelity cliff

**FACT:** Matusik & Hill (1998, *Academy of Management Review, 23*(4), 680–697, doi:10.5465/amr.1998.1255633) — contingent work affects knowledge accumulation and advantage; beyond cost/flexibility, it can create/upgrade knowledge stocks — and permeable boundaries imply leakage risk.

**FACT:** Argote & Ingram (2000, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 82*(1), 150–169, doi:10.1006/obhd.2000.2893) — knowledge in **people–task–tool interactions** is hardest to transfer across contexts; embedding there enables internal transfer while impeding easy external copy.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Panelists hold firm-specific seal judgments. Cliff-cut lets that reservoir walk out in *people* without landing in *tools/routines*. Require a **capture gate** (discord exemplars, seal-checklist diffs, arbitration notes) before `contract_parallel_run_pass` revoke — not a Loom thank-you.

**Kill:** Invoice-end ≡ knowledge-end. **Survive:** Capture before revoke. **Wound:** Never market “contractors make us smarter” as ACT ads; claim only that **uncaptured exit predicts repair cliffs**.

## CLIV.7 Organizational knowledge depreciates — surge learning does not bank forever

**FACT:** Argote, Beckman, & Epple (1990, *Management Science, 36*(2), 140–154, doi:10.1287/mnsc.36.2.140) — production knowledge **depreciates rapidly**; cumulative-output learning measures **overstate persistence**.

**FACT:** Darr, Argote, & Epple (1995, *Management Science, 41*(11), 1750–1762, doi:10.1287/mnsc.41.11.1750) — service franchise learning-by-doing also **depreciates rapidly**; transfer appears within same-franchisee stores, not across owners.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Dual-surge norms fade after demob. Post-exit monitors on panel-touched cells are **depreciation hedges**, not Demob Score™. FILLETA must not treat last week’s dual throughput as durable capacity.

**Kill:** “Scaled once → forever dual-ready.” **Survive:** Plan re-ramp cost next spike (pairs PANELRAMP). **Wound:** Borrow *depreciation speed*, not industry half-lives as product constants.

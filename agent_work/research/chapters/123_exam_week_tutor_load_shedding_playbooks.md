## CXXIII.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-BURNWARN already says: fidelity dips + JD-R redesign beat Burnout Score™ theater. SAFE-DDSCHED already says: hold equipped mix; ease load not standards; Protect+probe in the final ~72h. What remains commercially underspecified is the **ops join under calendar surge**: parents book more, students panic-request homework dumps, tutors accept “just this week” pour sessions, and marketplace logic celebrates hours-booked while brief-skip and soft-wrong avoidance spike.

Competitors resolve crunch wrongly: (1) **always-on** — take every session, sell availability as care; (2) **wellness email** — mindfulness tip while caseload doubles; (3) **hire flood** without coach bandwidth (demand bomb); (4) **cram costume** — tutor becomes human Chegg for “exam support”; (5) **soft standards** for anxious personas under deadline. MindCraft’s wedge is a **load-shedding playbook** that protects thinking minutes the same way hospitals protect ICU beds: reverse-triage low-acuity demand, cancel/defer electives, keep resources for cases that change outcomes.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Exam week is a predictable surge. Treating it as surprise heroism (everyone works harder) is a design failure. Honest companies publish what they *stop* doing so tutors can still make students think.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. Hours-booked / session-volume as exam-week North Star.  
2. Load Shed Score™ / Crunch Minutes / Availability Score™ as brand.  
3. Wellness-module-as-primary when the demand spike is caseload.  
4. Uncapped “we’ll take anyone who needs us” during Protect+probe.  
5. Hire surge without restoring coach/QA bandwidth.  
6. Tutor pour-mode / answer-dump as “exam support.”  
7. Soft standards / skip-challenge for crunch personas.  
8. ACT / score guarantees from crunch playbooks or late-night packing.

## CXXIII.2 Constructs (shed ≠ abandon; surge ≠ heroics)

| Construct | Meaning | MindCraft analogue | Failure mode |
|-----------|---------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Surge demand** | Predictable spike near exam dates | Parent bookings; HW-help flood; prove-week mocks | Surprise heroics |
| **Load shed** | Deliberately stop/defer low-leverage work | Pause elective Map tours; defer low-acuity HW dump; AI-scaffold first | Abandon students |
| **Reverse triage (borrowed)** | Free capacity by releasing low-risk demand | Route stable practice to Practice+coach; keep HITL for FEI-critical | Hospital cosplay brand |
| **Acuity** | Likelihood session changes FEI path | Soft-wrong streak + bridge gap + exam-horizon | First-come booking |
| **Elective cancel** | Defer non-essential ops under surge | Lore walks; admin meetings in crunch window | Cancel FEI itself |
| **SAFE-LOADSHED** | Named crunch playbook | This chapter | Hours-booked NS |

**Operational definition (HYPOTHESIS):** An exam-week load-shed is *SAFE-LOADSHED complete* when (a) triggers are calendar + demand/resource signals (days-to-exam band + caseload/fidelity alerts), (b) a published playbook **names what pauses** (elective Map tours, unbounded HW dumps, non-FEI admin, uncapped new intakes), (c) remaining HITL minutes prioritize **high-acuity FEI** (equipped struggle, bridge joins, labeled prove probes) over comfort greens, (d) AI/Solver scaffolds absorb low-acuity homework under SAFE-HWHELP rails — not as Chegg dump, (e) org redesign (caps, redistribute, pause hire without coaches) runs before wellness theater, and (f) success = fidelity@crunch + `transfer_pass` / `solo_transfer_pass` / brief-open rates — never hours-booked or Load Shed Score™.

## CXXIII.3 Exam accountability raises educator stress and can warp instruction — crunch is not neutral

**FACT (test-based accountability → stress + counterproductive practices):** von der Embse, Schoemann, Kilgus, Wicoff, & Bowler (2016, *Educational Psychology, 37*(3), 312–331, doi:10.1080/01443410.2016.1183766) — SEM on N=7,281 educators: educators who saw tests as invalid measures of effectiveness reported higher test stress and greater use of **counterproductive instructional practices** (including fear appeals) intended to motivate test-taking.

**FACT (efficacy buffers; stress still real):** von der Embse, Sandilos, Pendergast, & Mankin (2016, *Learning and Individual Differences, 50*, 308–317, doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.08.001) — teacher self-efficacy moderated links between test-stress and job satisfaction; efficacy support helps but does not erase accountability pressure.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Parent/tutor crunch culture that rewards fear appeals, pour-mode, and “grind tonight” is the edtech analogue of counterproductive practice. SAFE-LOADSHED must **block** those defaults — not celebrate them as hustle.

**Kill:** Fear-appeal / pour-as-care exam marketing. **Survive:** Crunch copy that names protected FEI behaviors and paused electives.  
**Wound:** School-accountability samples ≠ near-peer tutors — LOADSHED-* must measure pour/brief-skip under ACT date spikes.

## CXXIII.4 Work intensification is a policy/org problem — not a character deficit

**FACT (workload / intensification synthesis):** Creagh, Thompson, Mockler, Stacey, & Hogan (2023, *Educational Review*, doi:10.1080/00131911.2023.2196607) — systematic synthesis: teacher workload intensification and time poverty are tightly linked to accountability, datafication, and performativity cultures; the literature repeatedly frames heavier work as a **system design** outcome, not individual laziness.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Exam-week “just do more sessions” without removing other demands repeats intensification. Load shed is the commercial translation of *zero-sum work design*: new crunch demand must displace named electives, or fidelity collapses (SAFE-BURNWARN / SAFE-WORKFORCE).

**Kill:** Character/grit framing for tutor crunch (“our tutors go the extra mile” as the product).  
**Survive:** Published displacement list + caseload caps tied to ACT horizon.

## CXXIII.5 Surge capacity lesson — free capacity by releasing low-acuity demand, not by burning staff

**FACT (reverse triage founding empirics):** Kelen, McCarthy, Kraus, et al. (2009, *Disaster Medicine and Public Health Preparedness, 3*(Suppl 1), S10–S16, doi:10.1097/DMP.0b013e3181a5e7cd) — across three capacity-constrained hospitals (N=3,491 patients), ~44% needed no critical intervention for 4 days and were suitable for early discharge; reverse triage contributed the **majority** of realized surge beds (~50–59%); most capacity appeared within 24–48 hours.

**FACT (pediatric reverse triage magnitude):** Kelen, Troncoso, Trebach, et al. (2017, *JAMA Pediatrics, 171*(4), e164829, doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.4829) — ~10.8% eligible for immediate low-risk reverse triage and ~13.2% by 96 hours; effect unit-concentrated; reverse triage is one tool among cancel-electives / open-bed strategies — not infinite capacity.

**FACT (systematic review stock-take):** De Bondt, Sabbe, & Pollaris (2023, *Frontiers in Disaster and Emergency Medicine*, doi:10.3389/femer.2023.1303809) — reverse triage often yields ~**20–30%** additional surge capacity (more combined with other strategies); early-discharge adverse events generally low when multidisciplinary gates apply; heterogeneity warrants caution.

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable analogy, not hospital brand):** Do **not** sell “disaster medicine for tutors.” Transferable mechanism: under surge, **release low-acuity demand** (defer/route/AI-scaffold) so scarce HITL minutes serve high-acuity FEI. Elective-cancel analogue = pause lore tours, unbounded Chegg-style HW, uncapped intakes, nonessential admin in Protect+probe.

**Kill:** Infinite-availability brand; hospital-cosplay marketing. **Survive:** Acuity queue + named pause list + caps/redistribute/AI rail.  
**Wound:** Clinical AE ≠ tutoring harm — LOADSHED-* defines adverse events as fidelity collapse / pour relapse / abandonment proxies.

## CXXIII.6 Org redesign and resource crafting beat person-only crunch coping

**FACT (org > person burnout interventions, reuse):** Panagioti et al. (2017, *JAMA Internal Medicine, 177*(2), 195–205, doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.7674) — organization-directed burnout interventions outperform physician-directed ones on average.

**FACT (JD-R stock-take, reuse):** Bakker & Demerouti (2017, *Journal of Occupational Health Psychology, 22*(3), 273–285, doi:10.1037/ocp0000056) — dual pathways: demands→impairment; resources→motivation; interventions must address both sides.

**FACT (job crafting longitudinal):** Tims, Bakker, & Derks (2013, *Journal of Occupational Health Psychology, 18*(2), 230–240, doi:10.1037/a0032141) — employees who crafted job resources increased structural/social resources over ~2 months; resource increase linked to higher engagement/satisfaction and lower burnout; crafting challenging demands showed direct well-being links.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Exam-week playbooks should (1) **cut demands** (caps, defer electives, reverse-triage low-acuity HW to SAFE-HWHELP rails) and (2) **add resources** (coach bandwidth, Map grain tools, clearer acuity queues) — optionally invite tutors to craft *within* the playbook (choose which low-acuity slots to shed first). Person-only “self-care Sunday” fails Panagioti when caseload is the spike.

**Kill:** Wellness tip as primary crunch response; Burnout Score™ during ACT week.  
**Survive:** Demand cut + resource add with tutor craft rights inside the shed list.

## CXXIII.8 Doctrine — SAFE-LOADSHED (provisional)

1. **Crunch is predictable surge** — calendar triggers the playbook; surprise heroics are a design bug.  
2. **Shed low-acuity demand** — reverse-triage analogy: free HITL capacity without burning staff (Kelen line; De Bondt et al.) — never hospital-cosplay ads.  
3. **Displace, don’t stack** — new crunch demand must cancel named electives (Creagh intensification lesson).  
4. **Protect FEI fidelity under load** — Map-brief, soft-wrong climate, equipped struggle stay; pour-mode is not “exam support” (von der Embse counterproductive practices).  
5. **Org redesign first** — caps/redistribute/coach bandwidth before wellness theater (Panagioti; SAFE-BURNWARN).  
6. **Resource craft inside the playbook** — tutors may prioritize shed order; they may not invent uncapped hours as virtue (Tims et al.).  
7. **Join SAFE-DDSCHED** — Protect+probe student calendar and tutor load shed are one system; ease load not readiness standards.  
8. **No Load Shed Score™ / hours-booked NS / ACT guarantees from crunch packaging**.

**Confidence:** High that uncapped crunch + pour-mode + wellness-only responses are commercially and pedagogically unsafe vs FEI. Medium on exact acuity ranks and % capacity freed in MindCraft (needs LOADSHED-*). High to kill hours-booked NS / hospital cosplay / ACT guarantees from playbooks regardless.

## CXXIII.10 So what for MindCraft commercially

- **Copy:** “In exam week we protect the minutes that make you think — and we pause the busywork that burns tutors out.” Never “always available” or “our tutors grind with you all night.”  
- **Product:** Exam-horizon playbook in tutor console: acuity queue, named pause list, caseload caps, HW→Solver rail, hire gated by coach_util.  
- **Positioning:** Against marketplace volume, bootcamp pour, wellness-as-fix; for surge-honest HITL that still ships FEI under ACT dates.  
- **Metric:** fidelity@crunch, brief-open, pour↓, `transfer_pass` / `solo_transfer_pass` — demote hours-booked, Load Shed Score™, Crunch Minutes.  
- **Kill list:** Infinite availability; pour≡exam support; wellness-primary under spike; hire without coaches; ACT guarantees from crunch packaging.  
- **Growth:** Parent/district trust packet includes crunch playbook (what pauses, what protects) — procurement honesty asset.

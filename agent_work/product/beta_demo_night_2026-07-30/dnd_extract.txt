• Minimum Viable Product
– Trying to put in All The Coolest Features is a waste of time and 
money if they don’t add value for the buyer.
• Don’t cram everything into your initial prototypes
– Test the critical few and add things in as time/money/need allow.
– You get clearer information with fewer variables.
– Remember that this is an iterative process!

and what is not?

Acknowledging the 
Cost of Change
© JL Ryan Consulting LLC 2026 Use or Reproduction Without Permission is Prohibited 

--- PAGE 36 ---
• What steps in your customer’s workflow must change to 
implement your solution?
– Use generic workflow diagrams and interviews to dig in
• Do they even want to change? Can they change?
– Are there systems they’re stuck with?
• How painful are those changes?
– “T-shirt sizing” here: small, medium, large
– Can you reduce or eliminate the pain points?
• Consider physical, mental, financial, other impacts?
Determine the Whole Picture

 1 hour, 1 day, 1 month delays

The Basics 2
•
 Create a database and identify critical components to 
evaluate
–
 Record company name, product name, and image or description
–
 List ~5 critical components for review
•
 What will your buyer consider when making a purchase?
•
 Need to separate technologies but not overwhelm yourself
•
 Consider availability of information
–
 Don’t forget about products or technologies that are close enough to 
be a threat in the future

The Basics 3
•
 Create a standardized assessment matrix
–
 Include each of your critical components, weighting them if 
necessary
–
 Identify scoring criteria using “T
 -
 shirt sizing”
• Ideal, acceptable, unacceptable
• Time to result: <5 minutes, 5-15 minutes, >15 minutes
• Size: smartphone, toaster, minifridge
•
 Grade each competitor and review results
–
 May need to add, remove, or adjust criteria

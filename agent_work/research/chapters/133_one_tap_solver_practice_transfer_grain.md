## CXXXIII.1 Why this chapter exists

SAFE-HWHELP and SAFE-CRUNCHCTA already ban becoming a nicer Chegg and require dual rails + follow-on *in principle*. Ops and UX still leak at the **last inch**: student gets unstuck, closes Solver, and never starts Practice — or must navigate concept pickers, level menus, and mission browsers until the deadline brain quits. That is **dump-and-quit with extra steps**.

Competitors resolve the inch wrongly: (1) **close-and-done** (“Homework finished!” toast); (2) **generic Practice home** with no edge carry-through; (3) **forced marathon** (10-item wall after every hint — backlash → help-avoidance); (4) **streak/XP bait** that celebrates assisted greens; (5) **Transfer Score™** dashboards. MindCraft’s wedge: **one tap → one (or few) edge-matched FEI item(s)** with Solver closed or denied, then a SAFE-SCHED return — the commercial proof that help was instrumental, not executive cosplay.

**FOUNDER BELIEF under audit:** Instrumental help without a transfer grain is unfinished pedagogy. Identity updates from *solo evidence*, not from fluent unstick moments.

**Claims we refuse as doctrine:**
1. Dump-and-quit / “You’re done!” after Solver as default close.  
2. Multi-step maze (re-pick topic → level → mission) as the only handoff.  
3. Transfer Score™ / Handoff Minutes / Follow-on Streak as North Star.  
4. Assisted Solver accuracy ≡ ready / exam-ready.  
5. Forced marathon Practice wall after every soft hint (undermines help-seeking).  
6. XP/streak celebration for help completion without solo grain.  
7. Silent collapse of Practice rail under crunch (SAFE-CRUNCHCTA kill retained).  
8. ACT / score guarantees from transfer-grain packaging.

## CXXXIII.3 Instrumental help is the transition — not the finish line

**FACT:** Nelson-Le Gall (1985, *Review of Research in Education, 12*, 55–90; ERIC ED275741 / chapter “Help-Seeking Behavior in Learning”) — reconceptualizes help-seeking as adaptive strategy; distinguishes **executive** (dependency-oriented: have someone attain the goal for you) from **instrumental** (mastery-oriented: obtain only the help needed to solve *yourself*). Instrumental help-seeking is framed as a mechanism of transition from other-regulation to self-regulation.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Solver that stops at “unstuck” without a self-regulation attempt leaves the Nelson-Le Gall transition incomplete. The one-tap grain is the product’s forced next beat of *self*-regulation — not a second SKU.

**Kill:** “Help complete = session complete.”  
**Survive:** Instrumental help → immediate self-attempt on same edge.  
**Wound:** 1980s classroom observational tradition ≠ ACT product A/B — method transfer only; XFERGRAIN-* must instrument solo events.

## CXXXIII.4 Learners misuse on-demand help; design must teach the exit

**FACT:** Aleven, Stahl, Schworm, Fischer, & Wallace (2003, *Review of Educational Research, 73*(3), 277–320, doi:10.3102/00346543073003277) — selective review of help seeking in interactive learning environments: effective help-seeking relates to better learning, but learners often **underuse or misuse** help facilities; metacognitive skill × system design interactions matter.

**FACT:** Roll, Aleven, McLaren, & Koedinger (2011, *Learning and Instruction, 21*(2), 267–280, doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2010.07.004) — Help Tutor metacognitive feedback on help-seeking errors in Geometry Cognitive Tutor improved help-seeking behavior; Study 2 (N=67) found improved help-seeking skills **transferred** to new domain content in the following month when support was off. Earlier ITS2006/Help Tutor work showed behavior gains without automatic domain-score magic — do not overclaim.

**Applied (FOUNDER BELIEF → testable):** MindCraft must teach the **exit from help** as hard as the entry. A primary one-tap “Prove this alone” is metacognitive scaffolding in the Aleven/Roll sense — it is not nagware if the grain is short and edge-matched. Buried “Practice” links are the product analogue of unused help design: system *offers* the good path and students never take it.

**Kill:** Optional footer link to Practice home.  
**Survive:** Primary CTA + telemetry on help-exit compliance.  
**Wound:** Help Tutor ≠ ChatGPT Solver; Roll transfer is help-*skill* transfer, not automatic ACT points — keep claims at FEI grain.

## CXXXIII.5 Smooth example→problem transitions beat abrupt pairs — and beat “help then nothing”

**FACT:** Renkl & Atkinson (2003, *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 15–22, doi:10.1207/s15326985ep3801_3) — cognitive-load account of structuring the **transition** from example study to problem solving; fading successively introduces problem-solving elements until learners solve independently.

**FACT:** Atkinson, Renkl, & Merrill (2003, *Journal of Educational Psychology, 95*(4), 774–783, doi:10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774) — fading + principle self-explanation prompts produced medium-to-large effects on near and far transfer without extra time vs abrupt example–problem pairs in their experiments.

**FACT:** Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, & Sweller (2003, *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 23–31, doi:10.1207/s15326985ep3801_4) — **expertise reversal**: techniques that help novices (heavy guidance) can hurt more expert learners; guidance should fade as expertise grows.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Instrumental Solver help is a *worked/partial* scaffold. Ending without a problem-solving beat is an incomplete Renkl transition. Ending with a 20-item wall after a soft hint is expertise-blind flooding. SAFE-XFERGRAIN = **one short faded step**: completion-style or full attempt on a *new* instance of the same edge, then schedule returns — not dump, not marathon.

**Kill:** Help-then-nothing; help-then-marathon.  
**Survive:** Short fade-consistent grain + SCHED return.  
**Wound:** Lab fading ≠ homework crunch psychology — measure quit/help-avoidance (CRUNCHCTA falsifier retained).

## CXXXIII.6 Assisted performance ≠ durable learning; solo transfer is the commercial proof

**FACT:** Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı, & Mariman (2025, *PNAS*, doi:10.1073/pnas.2422633122) — ~1,000 HS math students; unfettered GPT Base raised practice scores but harmed later **unassisted** exams; guarded Tutor largely neutralized harm but did not beat no-AI learning. Students used Base as a crutch; perception ≠ exam learning.

**Already company law (XXXIII RT / SAFE-HWHELP):** Guards ≠ gain; assisted ≢ ready. This chapter densifies the **UX hinge**: without a one-tap solo grain, MindCraft cannot even *observe* Bastani’s unassisted outcome inside the product loop.

**Kill:** Solver-open accuracy as identity/readiness metric.  
**Survive:** `solo_transfer_pass` as required proof class after instrumental help when feasible.  
**Wound:** PNAS classroom GPT ≠ MindCraft Map-wrapped Solver — still the right commercial falsifier shape.

## CXXXIII.7 Desirable difficulty after help: retrieve, don’t re-read the hint

**FACT:** Bjork & Bjork (2020, *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition, 9*(4), 475–479, doi:10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.09.003) — desirable difficulties (spacing, variation, testing/generation, reduced feedback) can slow acquisition performance while improving long-term retention/transfer; learning ≠ performance during instruction.

**FACT (spacing spine, already SAFE-SCHED):** Distributed practice supports longer retention horizons than massing; overnight “I just saw the hint” fluency is a performance illusion if no retrieval follows.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Re-opening the Solver transcript or re-reading the hint is **restudy**. The transfer grain is **retrieval/generation**. One-tap must land on an *attempt* surface with coach/Solver closed — not on a second explanation monologue (SAFE-EXPLAIN).

**Kill:** “Review the solution again” as primary post-help CTA.  
**Survive:** Generate/attempt CTA + delayed return.  
**Wound:** Desirable difficulty under high anxiety needs SAFE-FADEX protect-then-fade — do not flood HMA learners with hard solo on day one of an edge.

## CXXXIII.8 Friction kills the handoff — one tap is pedagogy, not laziness

**FACT:** Johnson & Goldstein (2003, *Science, 302*(5649), 1338–1339, doi:10.1126/science.1091721) — defaults dominate outcomes; no-action paths impose switching costs (already SAFE-CRUNCHCTA).

**FACT:** Gollwitzer (1999, *American Psychologist, 54*(7), 493–503, doi:10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493) — implementation intentions (“When *x*, I will *y*”) automate goal pursuit at the cue.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Multi-step Practice navigation after help is a **hostile default** toward dump-and-quit. One-tap is the product’s implementation intention: *When instrumental help completes, I will attempt one solo item on this edge.* Optional buried links fail Gollwitzer because the cue (help-complete) is not bound to a single response.

**Kill:** “Find this topic in Practice” as handoff.  
**Survive:** Pre-bound CTA carrying edge IDs into `launchMissionDirect`-style session.  
**Wound:** One-tap convenience ≠ guaranteed transfer — still need item quality (SAFE-GENQ/FIGKEY) and closed-tool rules.

## CXXXIII.9 Product rules (SAFE-XFERGRAIN)

1. **Primary CTA after instrumental unstick** — “Prove this alone (1–3 items)” as first button; demote “Done / Close.”  
2. **Edge carry-through** — Pass conceptId + ingredient/FormatId + level recommendation; no re-picker maze.  
3. **Short default grain** — 1–3 items; opt-in “keep going”; ban mandatory marathon after soft hints.  
4. **Solo gate** — ≥1 item with Solver/AI reveal denied or closed (`solo_transfer_pass` eligible).  
5. **Near ≠ clone** — New instance / isomorphic variant; not the identical helped stem.  
6. **SCHEDULE chip** — After near pass (or honest miss), surface next SAFE-SCHED return; no “cleared forever.”  
7. **Crunch policy** — Help allowed; close path must still *offer* grain (soft-require default; measure avoidance).  
8. **Parent copy** — “Unstuck tonight → proved alone on the same edge” (SAFE-PDASH / CRUNCHMSG); never tonight-% NS.  
9. **Telemetry** — `followon_start`, `xfer_near_attempt`, `xfer_near_pass`, `xfer_return_due`, FEI; demote Transfer Score™ / Handoff Minutes.  
10. **Copy:** “You got unstuck — now prove the same edge alone.” Never “AI finished your homework” or ACT guarantees from handoff UX.

**Confidence:** High — Nelson-Le Gall instrumental transition + Aleven/Roll help-design + Renkl/Atkinson fade transition + Bastani unassisted harm + Bjork learning≠performance + Johnson/Gollwitzer friction/cue binding as method sources. Medium — MindCraft 1–3 item default and soft-require vs hard-gate under crunch (needs XFERGRAIN-*). High — dump-and-quit / maze / Transfer Score™ / assisted≡ready as commercially toxic under FEI + HWHELP/CRUNCHCTA.

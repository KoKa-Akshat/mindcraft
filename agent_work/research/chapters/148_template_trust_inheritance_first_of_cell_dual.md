## CXLVIII.3 Item models justify *family*-level work — not seal-free clones

**FACT:** Gierl & Lai (2012, *International Journal of Testing, 12*(3), 273–298, doi:10.1080/15305058.2011.635830) — automatic item generation uses **item models** (templates) whose elements are manipulated to produce many items; they discuss item-model banks and statistical procedures that can estimate parameters of generated items **without exhaustive separate pilot testing of every instance**.

**FACT:** Gierl, Lai, & Turner (2012, *Medical Education, 46*(8), 757–765, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2923.2012.04289.x) — three-stage AIG (cognitive model → item model → generation); content specialists structure the model; computers systematically combine elements — quality lives in the **model**, not in post-hoc fluency of each stem.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Figured FormatId generation is AIG economics. Blueprint dual is the model-level C4 investment. Inheritance is “don’t re-pilot every instance” — **not** “skip instance seals.” FIGKEY/GENQ still verify each clone.

**Kill:** “Template exists ⇒ clone is C4-ready.” **Survive:** Inherit *reviewer intensity* only; seals stay per-instance.

## CXLVIII.4 Family calibration saves resources — and still models clone variability

**FACT:** Sinharay, Johnson, & Williamson (2003, *Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics, 28*(4), 295–313, doi:10.3102/10769986028004295) — item families (e.g., AIG instances from models) require models that account for **within-family dependence**; they fit a Bayesian hierarchical model and introduce the **family expected response function (FERF)**; calibrating families (rather than every item separately) is framed as a path to **resource savings**.

**FACT:** Glas & van der Linden (2003, *Applied Psychological Measurement, 27*(4), 247–261, doi:10.1177/0146621603027004001) — item cloning induces **parameter variability** across clones; multilevel IRT treats clone parameters as draws from a family distribution; CAT can select a **family** then randomly administer one clone.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Family-as-unit supports not burning dual minutes on every sibling — and the same literature says clones vary. Inheritance = lower dual *intensity* after blueprint trust; instance seals + sample dual catch Glas/van der Linden variability. Not “parameters/seals copy-pasted.”

**Kill:** FERF ads as seal-free / always-dual-certified C4. **Survive:** Family unit for intensity; seals + sample for variability. **Wound:** LSAT/CAT ≠ gap-scan Map — borrow logic, not CAT marketing.

## CXLVIII.6 Sampling after demonstrated quality — not zero inspection

**FACT (transfer, with limits):** ISO 2859-1:1999 — attribute sampling indexed by AQL lets orgs inspect **samples** of lots rather than 100% when holding a process average near an acceptance quality limit; switching rules move among normal / tightened / reduced inspection as quality history changes (also reflected in ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 practice).

**Transfer limits:** Factory lots ≠ promote tickets. Borrow only: after demonstrated quality, **100% dual is not the only integrity tool** — sample + tighten-on-failure beats forever-100%-inspect.

**Applied (HYPOTHESIS):** Blueprint dual ≈ process qualification; inherited singles ≈ reduced inspection with revoke when sample dual or repair spikes. No ISO/AQL Score™ or factory-cosplay ACT ads.

**Kill:** Zero second-rater forever after one dual. **Survive:** Written sample % + revoke. **Wound:** Exact ISO tables ≠ product law — set rates in ops policy; pre-register TMPTRUST-*.

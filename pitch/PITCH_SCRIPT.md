## Elevator (2 minutes)

Most students end up working alone on something hard — a problem set, a paper, a presentation, code that will not compile. You Google it. You ask an AI and it hands back an answer to a slightly different question. Office hours are two days and one deadline away.

MindCraft is office hours from your room. Open the platform, show what you are actually stuck on, and a college mentor who has done this before is suddenly in the room with you — not locked to one subject, not scheduled days out.

The deepest, most built-out part of that today is Solver, our math and ACT vertical: we map the real gap first, wrap practice inside a story world so the work has stakes, and brief a tutor so they never start on page one. It is proof of what a fully-built vertical looks like on this platform.

I am Akshat — eight years watching students work alone at a tutoring table. Blake builds the engine underneath. We are not ChatGPT with a mentor badge; diagnosis, story, and a real person start from the same map.

Tonight we are asking for introductions: students stuck on real work, college mentors ready to help outside class, and yes — ACT parents and counselors, one channel among several.
